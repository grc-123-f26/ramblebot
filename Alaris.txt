Intro
        The world of Alaris has been an idea in my head for a long while. Ever since I started getting into fantasy through books, video games, and of course Dungeons and Dragons, I have always wanted to dream up my own world. A world that I could fill with history of my own design, one with creatures of my choosing, and most of all, a place I could tell my own stories in. As I would consume this media, I found myself wishing to change certain things. “This creature is super interesting, but what if it…” “That magic system is so cool, but what if it instead came from…”. These thoughts and ideas eventually solidified together, and I decided I wanted to make a fantasy setting of my own.
        In April 2021, I decided to start writing down my ideas, and got a solid ~18 pages of world information. Here is where I decided on how my world would look and feel, what its history would be, what kinds of creatures would inhabit it, and how its magic would work. This is where I decided on the name, one which I picked solely because I thought it sounded fantasy-like. It was admittedly rough, but getting my ideas finally onto paper (or in this case, a Google Doc) gave me a sense of satisfaction in my creation. This inspired me to continue trying to piece together the world, one little piece at a time.
        Now, just about 2 years later, I’ve come to a point where I have written enough material about Alaris that I feel a new collection of my writings should be compiled. I’m sure no one really cares too much about why I made Alaris, so I’ll leave one last personal note to keep in mind. I’m proud of what I’ve been able to create so far, and I feel like I have put a good amount of thought into this world. Just because this world has grown very dear to me does not mean I am above criticism. I welcome any and all comments or complaints you may have about the setting of Alaris. Heck, I love answering questions about my world, and if you have a suggestion for something to add, I would adore being able to share a piece of this world with you. So please, no matter what, share your thoughts. Thanks for reading, I dare you to have half as much fun reading this as I did writing it!                                                                                                               -Deercan

Setting and Tone
        I created Alaris with a very specific tone in mind. While certain parts of the world can certainly have aspects of other fantasy settings, Alaris as a whole mostly fits within the “Vanilla Fantasy” tone; meaning it adheres to most tropes of the fantasy genre without many radical changes from the formula. There are fearsome dragons, dangerous dungeons, scheming goblins, brave heroes, etc. I find this straightforward tone an excellent one for my purposes. This presentation as Vanilla Fantasy not only helped me write the world in a way I understood well but I also believe that it makes it easily accessible, and instantly familiar to most fans of the fantasy genre. Odds are, if you can think of a standard fantasy trope, Alaris has it in one form or another.
        The world of Alaris is, in its simplest form, a world of opportunity and adventure. Most folk are happy enough with their lives, but those who are bold enough to step up and become heroes (or sometimes villains) have many chances to prove themselves and change the course of the world. The world of Alaris is bright and hopeful, but rarely whimsical. Even during the night, The Mage Moon shines down on the world, illuminating the surface with its glow. Don’t let this fool you, though. There is evil in this world, dwelling in dark caverns and dread keeps, where even the brightest lights can struggle to shine.
        Magic is uncommon in Alaris, but not exceedingly rare. Most people are not dependent on advanced technology or magic for their daily lives. The technology of this world is mostly medieval. Most common folk have a very limited education, though nearly all can read and write. 
Several vicious monsters call Alaris home, and there will always be the evil among society who are dangerous. The world is not carefree by any means, but where there are villains, heroes will someday arise. There may be times of difficulty, danger, and even bloody wars, but the inhabitants of Alaris always keep pushing on, hoping for better days and a brighter future.

The History of Alaris
        The world of Alaris has a fairly long history, which spans around 10,000 years. Much has happened and changed since the world’s creation, as the eras have passed by. This world's history is written in (likely a little too much) detail in this section, which is divided by the four main eras. Each entry in this timeline is an important historical event, and includes a description of the event, as well as the year it occurred.
The First Era
(0-4024)
        
        The First Era, as the name implies, was the first and oldest known time to ever exist. Very few know any truths about The First Era beyond vague tales and half-remembered or half-invented events that occurred in that time so long ago. The First Era began with the creation of the prime races, and was brought to a close with the signing of The 3-Fold Pact.
        The concept of stable, quantifiable time is a construct of mortal races, and thus, their creation marks “Year Zero,” even though there was sentient life before the prime races were created. There is no definitive way to tell how long Alaris existed before the prime races.
        This era was marked most definitively by The Great War of The Ancestors, a military strife which lasted nearly the entirety of the time period. Many events that took place in The First Era happened because of this great conflict, and the era itself was a time of suffering and strife between the prime races.

Creation
(0)
        The First Era began with The Divine. No one knows where The Divine came from, or how they got their powers. The Divine was a sentient consciousness with supreme power over all things. They created and destroyed, implemented laws and broke them. The Divine was all things, and had power over all things.
        Alaris was created by The Divine, and along with it they created dragons and giants. These two races held great power and were the only sentient beings in Alaris for some time. Dragons and giants quickly became enemies of one another. While dragons discovered and hoarded things of beauty, becoming the first to hold precious metals and gemstones, giants learned and developed the art of Rune Magic, being the first creatures to harness the natural magic of Alaris.
Created a time after dragons and giants, the prime races were brought into this world. The prime races were created as independent, sentient creatures. The Divine created elves, humans, dwarves, and orcs to live within the world they had designed. The prime races populated the lands of Alaris, and slowly began to develop societies and cultures.
The Construction of The Godspire
(682-792)
        As the prime races advanced, they set about building a monument to honor The Divine. Coming together, the prime races spent several years creating a great tower in tribute to The Divine. They named this tower “The Godspire.” 
To properly pay tribute to The Divine, the prime races created 10 segmented layers of The Godspire, each floor representing a different aspect of their all-encompassing deity. To represent The Divine’s powers of life, they created a verdant garden, teeming with living animals and plants. To tribute The Divine’s powers of war, they constructed an arena, where their best warriors would fight to the death. Many years would pass until the completion of The Godspire.
________________
The Shattering
(792)
        Upon completing The Godspire, members of the prime races called to The Divine, and offered their tower as a tribute to their deity. The Divine accepted, and coalesced into physical form to take residence within The Godspire.
        Because the prime races had stratified The Godspire, The Divine themself was violently separated upon entering the tower. This event was a massive shift in the powers of Alaris, causing The Divine to be shattered into several different “Divine Aspects”, each inhabiting a layer of The Godspire. Each Divine Aspect had their own consciousness and a piece of The Divine’s Power.
        Each Divine Aspect had a title and an identity, although none carried the memories of The Divine. After The Shattering, The Divine Aspects were:
* The Allmother, Aspect of Life (Female)
* The Illuminator, Aspect of Light (Male)
* The Judging Hand, Aspect of Law (Genderless)
* The Tranquil Heart, Aspect of Peace (Genderless)
* The Essence of Nature, Aspect of Elements (Male)
* The Patron of Labor, Aspect of Trade (Male)
* The Master of Aether, Aspect of Magic (Female)
* Shadowhand, Aspect of Darkness (Genderless)
* Father of Battle, Aspect of War (Male)
* The Reaper, Aspect of Death (Female)


________________


Much Divine Power was lost during the shattering, and the omnipotent Divine was no more. In their place were left fragmented aspects, each with differing motives and designs. Each Aspect had great power over their domains, but each only wanted to further their own goals, and often used their powers to contest each other. The Reaper could strike dead whoever She wished, unless The Allmother protected them. The Tranquil Heart could have all beings living in harmony, unless The Father of Battle stirred up strife. In this way, The Divine Aspects served as balances of power for each other, ensuring no one Aspect would hold total control of Alaris.
The prime races were appalled by this result, and began to blame one another for the loss of their all-powerful deity. Many of them, seeing that The Divine Aspects now resided in a physical location, wished to claim The Godspire as their own.
The Shattering also had the effect of creating The Shadowfell and The Feywild, which were distorted reflections of The Material Plane. From these planes came Fey, mystical creatures which associate with emotions. The Seelie Fey were creatures of mirth, bravery, and whimsicality. The Unseelie Fey were monsters of sorrow, rage, and cold numbness.

The Great War of The Ancestors
(800-4024)
        The Great War of The Ancestors began in earnest when the prime races came to conflict over laying claim to The Godspire. Over the long years of the war, The Godspire would change hands multiple times, being claimed by the dwarves for a time, before the humans invaded, then fell to the elves. The prime races wanted to be near The Godspire, so that they could petition The Divine Aspects for their aid.
        This constant, multi-generational conflict gave great losses and gains to all sides. The engine of war kept Alaris changing and developing slowly yet surely, with innovations in warfare, arcane magics, and all other kinds of technology.
        The only exception to this constant warfare were the orcs. Most orcs did not wish to fight over The Godspire, and so retreated, settling in fertile lands and valleys, where they lived as mostly-peaceful tribes
________________
The Great Diaspora
(1024-1025)
        While The Great War of The Ancestors was only just beginning, the elves had a schism in their culture. Many elves believed they should abandon the war and seek their own peaceful place in the world, while others thought the idea of abandoning their rightful homeland was unacceptable.
        The current Highwizard of the elves, named Yvellim, was in opposition to the war. Using magical secrets that were all but unknown at the time (Indeed, many techniques and arcane symbols that were used are not yet understood at the present time), Yvellim created an enormous spell circle 100 feet in diameter. The sheer scope and complexity of the spell circle allowed it to channel the Aether in Alaris towards a powerful effect.
        The sudden and inexplicable disappearance of every elf in Alaris was shocking to other members of the prime races, although they were quick to capitalize on their absence.  Humans and dwarves began flooding into the elven lands, establishing colonies and claiming the territory for themselves.
        After a little more than a year, the elves were able to devise a way back to The Material Plane. Returning, they found their lands overtaken by their foes. It took several hundreds of years to reclaim all of their stolen land. This constant struggle and strife ingrained a strong tradition for combat within the elves.
        The effects of their year-long stay in The Feywild has left a trace of fey magic in every elf today, passed down through generations. The elves that did not return to their home in The Material Plane went on to embrace the fey magic around them, becoming Eladrin. 
During this time, a power hungry elf named Ithma convinced some of her peers to seek out other planes with her. Hungry for power and opportunity, they left Feywild in favor of The Shadowfell, feasting themselves on the dark powers which they found there. Ithma appointed herself Raven Queen, and her followers eventually became the shadar-kai.
The Adaptation of Runic Magic
(1374)
        As The Great War of The Ancestors raged ever on, the dwarves within their mountain homes sought help from the giants. The giants decided to aid them, and taught the most respected craftsman among the race the secrets of runic magic. These techniques allowed the dwarves to begin creating the world’s first magical items, objects which were able to manipulate Aether, even in the hands of an unskilled wielder.
        Using this knowledge, the dwarves began to create magical weapons and armor, which they used to help the war effort. As time passed, other races began to discover the secrets of enchanting magical objects, although the dwarves have remained the undisputed masters of the craft to this day.

The Construction of Faer-Belthor
(1827)
During The Great War of The Ancestors, a group of elves began experimenting with dark forms of necromancy that dealt in the realm of souls. These elves created terribly powerful weapons of war, which they aimed to use in the war effort. Foremost among these weapons was a special cannon named “Faer-Belthor.” This cannon was fueled by the souls of the fallen, be them friend or foe, and fired beams of powerful energy that completely annihilated everything that it touched.
Faer-Belthor was only used once, to great effect, and destroyed an entire army of dwarves, along with their village and the mountain behind them (Which can be seen to this day, now called The Sundered Peak). The other elves were so distraught by the terrible power of the weapon, they destroyed it, and executed those that created the weapon. Some elves protested this, claiming that they could use this technology to win the war.
Eventually, the elves that supported the creation of other weapons like Faer-Belthor were banished from their homelands, and retreated to the underdark, where they created a civilization. Their newly-formed society was created on the idea of constructing more weapons like Faer-Belthor, and using them to exact vengeance on their surface-dwelling cousins.
These elves that made the underdark their home became known as drow, and the vile magic that they experimented gave them a drastically different appearance from surface-dwelling elves, as well as innate magical powers.
________________
The Creation of Dragonborn
(2113)
        As time passed in the world, dragons began to desire servants to make their life more convenient. To this end, they asked The Allmother, Aspect of Life, to grant them the ability to make a sentient race. The Allmother granted their request, and the dragons created dragonborn from their own blood.
        Each color of dragon, metallic or chromatic, created dragonborn in their own image. They gave unto their creations a small amount of their own power. This granted them innate breath weapons, but also bestowed into dragonborn a personality similar to their creators.
This personality given to dragonborn proved to be a mistake, as their demeanors would clash with that of their draconic masters, causing much strife between them. Chromatic dragonborn pushed back against their masters violently, gaining their freedom through bloody revolts. Metallic dragonborn gained freedom through generations of careful planning, paving a way for their descendants to escape their masters safely.
Eventually, dragons were forced to admit the creation of dragonborn was a failed endeavor, and within only a few centuries of their creation, no dragonborn in Alaris bowed to a draconic master.
________________
The Creation of Halflings
(2490)
        As The Great War of The Ancestors was becoming ever more violent and deadly, The Allmother wanted to show the prime races that there was an alternative to endless war. Together with The Illuminator, she created the race of halflings.
        Halflings were created to live pleasant, quiet, and peaceful lives, which they were naturally inclined to do. Halflings formed communities across Alaris, and almost all of them stubbornly refused to fight. Through their friendly and accommodating attitudes, halflings  showed several members of the prime races that there was more to life than endless struggle.
Some consider this point in history to be the turning point of The Great War of The Ancestors. Halflings proved to the world that cooperation was always possible, and nearly 1,500 years after their creation, Teno the halfling would host the signing of The 3-Fold Pact.
________________
The Dwarven Rune Schism
(3357)
As the dwarves continued to master rune magic, a few clans wanted more. They were unsatisfied with only enchanting weapons, and sought to enchant their very bodies. Several dwarves thought this was a misuse of the gift that giantkind gave them, while others were eager to develop this branch of rune magic for use in the war. Eventually, the two opposing sides of the argument broke off, those who wished to try this new type of runic magic splitting from The Mountainhomes, creating their homes in the deep tunnels of the underdark
Eventually, those dwarves who dwelled in the underdark brought life to their goals, creating magical branding irons that could give their bodies innate magical powers. These flesh-enchanting dwarves became known as duergar, and their appearance has shifted further and further from their dwarvish cousins. Giants saw these flesh runes as abhorrent, and grew to despise duergar deeply.


________________
The Creation of Gnomes
(3518)
        Over the nearly 3,000 years of war that had passed at the time, very few innovations had been made outside of warfare. The prime races’ focus on The Great War of The Ancestors had halted the progression of technology in nearly every other field. To remedy this, The Allmother and The Master of Aether worked together to create the gnomes. Gnomes were brought into the world with a strong sense of invention, and a drive for innovation. Within only a few short years of their creation, gnomes had created several breakthroughs in agriculture, infrastructure, arcane studies, and education.
        Gnomes would be integral to the progression of technology in Alaris, and many attribute the rise of The Old Empire to the innovations of the gnomes. Even into modern times, gnomes are known as great inventors. While Dwarves were the first of the mortal races to use rune magic to create magical items, gnomes pushed the art to its limits, and have created many wondrous and spectacular magic items using rune magic.
The Rise of The Old Empire
(3812)
        Humans, being the shortest-lived and most ambitious race among the prime races, had never been truly unified under a common flag or kingdom, until the founding of The Old Empire. The Old Empire was a feudalistic totalitarian organization which was formed by several prominent human leaders. The foremost among these leaders became nobility, and they elected an emperor to unify and lead them.
        Thenmar Rem was the first emperor, and he and his successors made great steps to unify humanity under a common goal: to win The Great War of The Ancestors. Humans flocked to the banner of The Old Empire, and submitted themselves to its castes and structures. Within a few generations, The Old Empire had solidified itself into a stable and powerful nation, which encompassed almost all of Alaris.
The 3-Fold Pact & Founding of The Heraldic Order
(4024)


        The Great War of The Ancestors served as a driving force for nearly every major event and change in Alaris, but even the most vengeful and power-hungry among the prime races eventually grew tired of constant warfare. A halfling named Teno managed to gather representatives of the prime races and bring them together to a peace meeting where they eventually came to an agreement. This became known as The 3-Fold Pact.
        Each race was to keep their currently occupied lands, and not encroach upon another’s territory. The 3-Fold Pact ensured cooperation and peace by balancing power as evenly as possible. The Godspire, which inspired much of the conflict in the war, would be held by a neutral party, named The Heraldic Order.
        The Heraldic Order was a neutral organization, which was not affiliated with any of the prime races or their factions. Any who wished could revoke their citizenship and join the order, dedicating their lives to interceding between The Divine Aspects and the mortal races.

The Era of Prosperity
(4024 - 9012)
The Era of Prosperity began with the signing of The 3-Fold Pact, and the peaceful times that followed it. With the end of The Great War of The Ancestors, the mortal races began to develop, grow, and progress. During this Era, the mortal races began to travel to other planes of existence, and adapt into new races. The number of new races that were created (be it by The Divine Aspect of Life, or through the influence of other planes) in this time period is notable.
As more sentient races were created by The Divine Aspects or the influence of other planes, the prime races ceased to wholly control Alaris, their importance waning as The Great War of The Ancestors became a faded memory. The Era of Prosperity would eventually come to an end with the banishing of The Godspire and the chaos that ensued, plunging Alaris into The Era of Change.


________________


The Conquest of The Orcish Lands
(4030)
Within the new, peaceful alliance of the prime races that was brought about by The 3-Fold Pact, many opportunists lurked about. Seeing the prime races come together, these schemers devised a plan to further solidify the alliance, while also making themselves wealthy. They manipulated the leaders of the prime races to turn their attention towards the orcish lands: strategically unimportant yet highly fertile valleys and meadows.
The members of The 3-Fold Pact combined their strength and took over the orcish lands, conquering the orcs that inhabited it. Though the orcs were fearsome fighters, their aloofness from The Great War of The Ancestors meant that their military technology had not advanced at the same rate as the prime races, and they were far behind. They were eventually driven out of their homes, retreating to the inhospitable mountain ranges, deserts, and tundras, where they can still be found.
Most of the land gained in this conquest was claimed by The Old Empire, although the dwarves and the elves also gained valuable territory. This conquest had the intended effect, solidifying the prime races in an alliance, as well as making them all much richer. Stories of this event in history are still told among the scattered orcish tribes, and many wish to retake their lands.

The Creation of Kobolds
(4613)
Since the “failed” creation of dragonborn, the dragons had not lost their desire for a race of servants. They again asked The Allmother for the ability to create sentient life, and she bestowed it to them. Using this power alongside the lessons they had learned through their previous failure, the dragons created a new race of servants, which they named Kobolds.
Kobolds were much smaller and physically weaker than dragonborn, and were given an innate sense of extreme dedication and resourcefulness, which the dragons found fitting for their servants. Kobolds were not given the blood of dragons, and so they lacked the powers and personality that led the dragonborn to rebel. To this day, many kobolds still diligently serve their draconic masters.

The Creation, Rise, and Fall of Lizardfolk
(4751-5584)
As time passed in Alaris, eventually swamps began to form in the land. The Allmother saw these swamps, and the teeming ecosystems which thrived within them. Seeing the abundance of life within the swamps, The Allmother bestowed a seed of sentience upon many of the inhabitants of the swamp, and watched to see how life within would change.
This gift of sentience caused the creatures to evolve and adapt rapidly, growing and changing. Their strong survival instincts caused them to be extremely competitive with each other, and very quickly the newly-sentient life of the swamps began to fight with each other. They fought over food, territory, and resources, brutally driving themselves to the brink of extinction. In the end, the lizardfolk emerged victorious, wiping out all other sentient races of the swamp, and securing for themselves the entire swamp.
Time passed as the Lizardfolk began to populate the swamp, using its resources freely and without competition. Their society was progressing quickly, but their overpopulation and exploitation of their lands caused a catastrophic failure in their civilization, as they began to fight over what little resources remained. This conflict caused the lizardfolk people to fall back into a tribal society, where they remain to this day, warring with other lizardfolk tribes over limited resources.

The Creation of Yuan-ti
(5708)
As The Old Empire spread its grasp over Alaris, a group of humans who wished for freedom away from the emperor's rule escaped to the marshes, where The Old Empire had not yet established control. These rebels found the marshes inhospitable, and so they turned their attention to Shadowhand, The Aspect of Darkness, and asked for the ability to survive in the marshes.
Shadowhand granted this boon, but they exacted a price from the rebels in exchange. Shadowhand gave the rebels serpentine adaptations to help them survive and thrive in the dangerous marshes, yet Shadowhand took their compassion in trade. This new race, called yuan-ti, were cruel and crafty, and disliked by nearly every other sentient race.
To avoid the scorn of others, yuan-ti made their homes deep beneath the mud and muck of the marshes. Their scheming nature left them constantly planning against other races, and their own kind. When The Godspire was Banished, Shadowhand was no longer able to withhold the yuan-ti’s compassion from them, although their culture has long since held ruthlessness as the most worthy attribute. To this day, they still live in their ancient temples beneath the marshes, crafting dastardly plots. Despite (or perhaps because of) their cruel society, some choose to leave and seek peace, or even adventure.

The Creation of The Tower of Wonders & The Birth of Artifice
(5979)
When The Great War of The Ancestors came to a close, and folk transitioned to simpler, more peaceful lives, many great (primarily gnomish) inventors found their innovations were no longer needed. In fact, as the war ended, common folk of Alaris no longer tolerated the often-dangerous experiments of these inventors. These innovators gathered together and made an expedition to a barren island within The Inner Sea.
        Here they constructed a tower in which they could freely practice their inventions without judgment or scorn. Eventually, news of this haven for daring inventors spread, and several other like-minded individuals took up residence on the island. Common folk paid these inventors and their tower little mind, until they unveiled the first fruits of their experiments.
        After 15 years of dedicated and isolated study, the occupants of the tower traveled to the mainland, and began to travel about Alaris, showing off their newest discovery: A new, practical form of magic called “Artifice.” This magic was arcane in nature, and was brought about by harnessing the power of dwarven runes in combination with mechanical engineering to manipulate Aether without having to memorize complicated components. These exhibitions shocked and amazed the people of Alaris, who praised the inventors for their daring genius, and named their home “The Tower of Wonders,” a title which exists to this day.

The Founding of The Circle of Scholars
(6045)
Though the common folk were enraptured by the magic that was artifice, the leaders of The Old Empire saw it as repulsive. They believed that artifice would give too much power to the common folk, and so they banned its practice within their borders. To compete with The Tower of Wonders, the leaders of The Old Empire founded The Circle of Scholars, a group that could research, innovate, and regulate all forms of arcane magic.
This newly-formed Circle of Scholars was a highly skilled conclave which made several breakthroughs in the art of wizardry, and were responsible for discovering the conventions and natural laws of arcane magic. Among the founders of The Circle of Scholars were several renowned wizards, many of whom created new spells to share with the world.
The founding of The Circle of Scholars brought about a golden age for wizards and other arcane spellcasters, and also regulated the practice to prevent misuse. In the time of The Old Empire, only the nobility had the privilege of learning magic, and all practitioners were required to submit to the authority of The Circle of Scholars.
This organization still exists today, and their role has changed little in The Kingdom of Goldhame. The Circle of Scholars still works towards innovating and regulating arcane magic, although they no longer deny common folk its practice.

The Creation of Kenku
(7214)
As the years passed by in The Material Plane, The Raven Queen had grown insatiably curious about its state of affairs. Because she had used the powers of The Shadowfell to sustain her life, The Raven Queen was unable to leave the plane, and was hesitant to send her shadar-kai subjects. To solve her problem, The Raven Queen took note of the dragons, and asked The Allmother for the power to create sentient life.
        Always willing to see new forms of life, as was her nature, The Allmother agreed, but only gave unto The Raven Queen a small piece of her power, as she was once a mortal, and could not be trusted with more. The Raven Queen took this seed of sentient life and worked for years to create a new race to serve her. The result was the kenku.
        The Raven Queen, using a raven as a starting point, created the kenku with excellent memories and an innate skill at mimicry, so that they could travel the planes and later report to her in exacting detail what they had experienced. Due to the limits of the power she was given by The Allmother, however, The Raven Queen had to remove most of their originality, and their ability to fly.
        Satisfied with her work, The Raven Queen released the kenku into the planes, to travel, learn, and report back to her. As time passed, several kenku returned to her, recalling what they had learned of the planes, although a few chose to remain abroad in the planes, forsaking The Raven Queen and her designs. The kenku of modern day often only know The Raven Queen from stories, and most live free lives.

The Creation of Goblinoids
(8451)
        With the passing of time, the mortal races continued to prosper, flourishing in The Era of Prosperity. During this period, the evil Divine Aspects of War, Darkness, and Death conspired together for a way to break the idyllic peace that gripped Alaris. Acting together, The Evil Aspects took The Allmother captive, and forced her to give them each a seed of sentient life. Having no other choice, The Allmother capitulated, and The Evil Aspects went about creating new races to serve their designs.
        These creatures were known as goblinoids, and their sudden arrival on Alaris proved a great threat to the common folk. These goblinoids were all directly controlled by an Evil Divine Aspect, and had no free will. The Evil Divine Aspects used these creatures as pawns in their schemes, and caused great chaos, suffering, and panic with the goblinoids as their tools.
        Goblinoids terrorized the inhabitants of Alaris indiscriminately and persistently, shattering the idyllic peace of The Era of Prosperity. Many historians believe that the creation of goblinoids, as well as the plethora of other sentient creatures that arose due to The Allmother’s actions, inspired the The Order of The Crystal Moon to banish The Godspire.


________________
The Banishing of The Godspire
(9012)


        As The Divine Aspects continued to influence Alaris in whichever way they saw fit, the threads of peace and prosperity in the world were beginning to unravel. The mortal races slipped ever-closer to another catastrophic war, and the simple stability that allowed The Era of Prosperity had begun to disappear. During this time, a mysterious group formed, naming themselves The Order of The Crystal Moon.
        This group believed that the Divine Aspects held far too much power over the mortal races and the world, and each had too flawed a view of their “perfect world”. The Order of The Crystal Moon, using powers and magic unknown to scholars even today, gathered together and banished The Godspire to a sealed dimension, where they could no longer directly see or influence the planes of Alaris.
        The Banishing of The Godspire proved to have world-altering effects, shifting the fabric of reality itself. After they had banished The Godspire, The Order of The Crystal Moon disappeared without a trace. The effects of their actions have caused several extreme changes, which can be seen even in modern day.
        The most obvious of these changes was the absence of The Divine Aspects. Sealed away in a hidden dimension, they could no longer see or act on any of the planes. Their power was dulled, and they were rendered near-entirely impotent. The Divine Aspects were able to find a workaround, and found they could connect their powers to the spark of a mortal.
        By imprinting a mortal’s spark in this way, Divine Aspects could grant them small amounts of their power, and give them vague communications through visions and dreams. This process radically alters the mortal’s spark, and leaves them permanently changed to reflect the Divine Power that they wield.
        
________________
The Banishing of The Godspire also had changes on several races. Goblinoids were no longer controlled as pawns by the evil Divine Aspects, and now had free will (though the decades of their culture still leads most on the path set by the evil Aspects). This major shift in reality also caused changelings to return to The Material Plane, freeing them from the clutches of The Raven Queen.
        The Banishing of The Godspire caused an upheaval of religion and society itself. The Heraldic Order found itself without a purpose, and were forced to reinvent themselves, choosing to dedicate their order towards carrying out the will of the good Aspects.
        This major world event can also be tied to the eventual fall of The Old Empire, which had structured much of itself around The Divine Aspects and their power. The Banishing of The Godspire proved to have long-spanning and reality-shaking effects for every creature in Alaris, and its advent ushered in The Era of Change.

The Era of Change
(9012-10,001)
The Era of Change was a radical time in Alaris, not as bloody as The First Era, yet not as peaceful as The Era of Prosperity. The Era of Change was heralded by The Banishing of The Godspire, which was the most world-shaking event since The Divine was shattered. The Era of Change marked an end to the interference of The Divine Aspects, and the massive impact they had on the mortal races. No longer did mortals have to live in fear of The Evil Aspects, and now they could not call on the aid of The Good Aspects.
The Era of Change is named for the massive changes and upheavals that affected the mortal races of Alaris. The Old Empire underwent the most change, although the other prime races experienced shifts in their lives as well. The Era of Change is the shortest historical time period, lasting only one thousand years, yet there were several important events that still pervaded The Era of Change.

The Journey of Narkur
(9015)
Within The Old Empire there was a half-elven explorer named Narkur, who secured a royal grant to sail across The Sea of Flames in search of islands to colonize. With the instability in The Old Empire, his journey was approved in an effort to distract the common folk from the still-recent disappearance of The Divine Aspects.
Narkur sailed The Sea of Flames, and made landfall on The Weilo Islands. Narkur met the local islanders, humans who had made the journey thousands of years prior. These islanders had been wholly isolated for thousands of years, and had developed their own culture and society away from the influence of others. Narkur traded with the locals for some native fruit, called kozae, and returned to The Old Empire to spread news of his success.
The kozae fruit was found to have special hallucinogenic properties when refined, and were sold to the nobility at a very high price. Excited by the prospect of a fortune, Narkur soon left The Old Empire to again journey to the islands and bring back more kozae fruit. He made this expedition several times, and secured a sizable fortune.
Narkur’s good luck ran out one stormy day, when he set sail upon The Sea of Flames with his treasure in the hold, planning to share his fortune with the islanders that enabled him to amass his wealth. His ship was tossed by the storms, however, and crashed onto a large, dangerous jungle island. This island was named Narkur’s End, and has gained a reputation for being cursed in modern day.

The Goldhame Coup & Civil War
(9492 - 10,000)
Nearly 500 years after The Banishing of The Godspire, The Old Empire’s days seemed to be numbered. An avaricious politician from the noble house Goldhame gathered his allies and planned a coup, to seat themselves on the throne. They made their plans and tried to execute them, intending to dethrone the emperor and his heir.
Their plans were not executed as they had hoped, however, and the coup became a bloody mess, ending the life of the heir and several other important leaders close to the throne, but failing to kill the emperor. Enraged, the emperor tried to throw the full weight of The Old Empire against the Goldhame house and their allies. The Old Empire, still brittle and weakened from The Banishing of The Godspire, began to collapse.
What followed was a bloody civil war as the emperor and house Goldhame clashed. Sides were chosen, armies were levied, and war was waged. The civil war proved long and costly for all sides, especially the common folk of The Old Empire. Whenever one side drew close to victory, the other would spring back by gaining the support of a new ally or sabotaging their enemies’ plans.
The Goldhame Civil War lasted slightly more than 500 years, and is still a fresh memory in the minds of most mortal races of Alaris in present day. The war tore apart families, lands, and communities, the scars of which are still seen today.

The Secession of The Coast of Hopes
(9745)
As The Old Empire was occupied with their civil war, a free-spirited port city on The Coast of Hopes took the opportunity to organize themselves and secede, becoming an independent city-state. The Old Empire tried to forcibly reclaim the rebellious city, yet they held firm, protecting their city and their territory. The city was the only settlement in The Old Empire that could properly harvest Ironwood trees, and they speedily constructed a fleet from the near-indestructible wood.
The city defended itself against The Old Empire, standing strong against their armies and fleets. With the chaos of The Goldhame Civil War, eventually The Old Empire was forced to let them be. Upon hearing news of their victory, citizens of the city had a celebration, releasing paper lanterns into the skies, to be carried off into the ocean breeze. The sight of these lanterns slowly gliding above the waves was so moving, the residents of the city decided to rename their home to signify their new freedom.
Sea-light City is still an independent city-state to this day, and is the only nation in Alaris with access to harvest Ironwood. Sea-light ships are known to be the best vessels money can buy, and are essential to safely sail across the craggy Sea of Spikes. Because of their independence, Sea-light city is known to all as a beacon of hope and freedom. In modern day, all are accepted into the city without regard for their past, so long as they behave themselves.

The Founding of The Freeswords
(9807)
As The Goldhame Civil War continued, an enterprising fighter by the name of Vajra Turik decided to found a mercenary company to profit off of the fighting. Naming her new company “The Freeswords,” Vajra hired several experienced and capable fighters, then offered her services to the highest bidder.
The Freeswords proved to be terrifyingly effective, and won several important battles. They were notoriously true to their word, and stuck to their contracts always. They only sold their services for a year at a time, however, and always assigned contracts to the highest bidder. Because of this, The Freeswords ended up fighting for both sides of The Goldhame Civil War several times, and becoming extremely rich in the process.
Eventually, Vajra retired with a sizable fortune, though The Freeswords still exist to this day. Though they no longer field large armies, The Freeswords are notorious for their integrity and efficiency. Only the most skilled or promising people are allowed to join, and they are held to a high standard. In modern times, The Freeswords hire out their members for smaller jobs, providing bodyguards, scouts, and capable squads to those that have enough gold.

The Infringement of The 3-Fold Pact
(9957)
As The Goldhame Civil War seemed to be drawing to a close with The Old Empire weakening, the then-emperor Hathis became desperate, looking for resources to fuel his armies. Looking for an unexpected advantage, Emperor Hathis sent raiding parties to the lands of the dwarves and elves, stealing valuable mithral and faetree sap. For a time, this ploy was successful, and the valuable resources made his armies far more formidable.
Eventually, the other races of The 3-Fold Pact saw through Hathis’ deception, and realized that The Old Empire had violated their millennium-old agreement. The elves and the dwarves were enraged by this, and moved swiftly to bring down The Old Empire.
Now fighting a war on three fronts, the rule of the emperor was coming to an inevitable end. The scars of this betrayal are still visible to this day, and the traitorous actions of humankind are still fresh in the memory of many of the longer-lived dwarves and elves.

The Grim Raze & The Fall of The Old Empire
(10,000)
        Though The Old Empire was at a considerable disadvantage, The Goldhame Civil War lasted another 150 years after their betrayal of The 3-Fold Pact. The Old Empire fought and struggled against its downfall vehemently, and many lives were lost on both sides. As the final years of the war were taking place, Emperor Rellick made a final stand against Goldhame, the dwarves, and the elves.
        Unable to admit defeat, yet unable to deny it, Emperor Rellick gave a final order to his armies: destroy everything of The Old Empire. Put farms to the torch, destroy temples and tear down libraries. The Imperial Palace collapsed, and the capital city was burned. Several soldiers rebelled against these orders, but still many obeyed.
        Emperor Rellick was so obsessed with keeping The Old Empire out of the hands of house Goldhame, he even spent the small sum left in the Imperial treasury to hire the services of dragons, paying them to destroy all they could of The Old Empire. Even the destructive might of the dragons was not enough to satisfy Rellick, so he turned to darker powers to fulfill his wishes.
The exact nature of his bargains with the fiends are unknown, but it is obvious that this “Grim Raze” had supernatural origins. During this time, mysterious plagues spread across The Old Empire, while plants and animals fell to blights. Several stories tell of the sun being eclipsed for weeks at a time, and rivers dried up suddenly.
         To this very day, scholars and historians lament the bitter orders of Emperor Rellick, and many curse him and his grave. Much of the vast knowledge and splendor of The Old Empire was destroyed in its fall.
        Grimly satisfied with his work, Emperor Rellick surrendered himself to the authority of house Goldhame, and was executed immediately for the crimes he committed against his own people. With his execution, The Goldhame Civil War came to an end, and with it, The Old Empire.


Post Reform
(10,001-)
The time known as Post Reform is the current Era of Alaris, and is still comparatively young. The fall of The Old Empire marked a new Era in the world, with one of the strongest and most influential factions in Alaris being near-completely destroyed. This new Era was dubbed “Post Reform” by Frederick Goldhame, who also decreed a restart of the global calendar, marking the founding of The Kingdom of Goldhame as the new “Year One”.
The Post Reform Era has, thus far, been marked by advancement and aspirations. The Kingdom of Goldhame hopes to reclaim the glory of The Old Empire, while new factions and organizations have arisen, looking to grow in wealth and power. No matter what happens as time in Alaris carries on, it is sure to bring about an opportunity for adventure, for those that are daring enough to seize it.

The Founding of The Kingdom of Goldhame
(10,001)
With the fall of The Old Empire, house Goldhame and its closest allies rose to power. Frederick Goldhame was crowned the king of this new government, which was named The Kingdom of Goldhame. Frederick’s closest allies were lifted to the station of Grand Dukes. In this way House Gauntier, House Tylock, and House Dimir came to power. House Goldhame was renamed to House Meldorus, and a statute was put into place, placing only the reigning monarch and their immediate family into House Goldhame.
This new government had several differences to The Old Empire, although The Kingdom of Goldhame has repurposed much of their tactics. The Kingdom of Goldhame is still primarily feudal, although all citizens, no matter how low in caste, have basic rights like travel, the ability to choose one’s own profession, protection against crimes, and the opportunity to rise in caste.
The Kingdom of Goldhame’s primary goal is to reclaim what was lost in The Grim Raze, and to reach the glory that The Old Empire once held. This is proving to be a very difficult task, especially as the elves and dwarves refuse trade and even contact with The Kingdom of Goldhame.

The Founding of The Shadow Network
(10,065)
As time passed in Alaris, certain individuals conspired together on a way to skirt around restrictive laws, forming The Shadow Network. This new organization was created to enrich all involved, and quickly spread its grasp into all areas of profit within The Kingdom of Goldhame and beyond. The Shadow Network began to dominate several criminal industries, including smuggling, common thievery, and assassinations.
This dark organization still exists today, its power ever-growing, yet remaining (mostly) outside of the public eye. Anyone who tries their hand at illegal activities soon finds themselves under the attention of The Shadow Network, and must make the choice to join them, or die.
Despite being composed almost entirely of criminals,  The Shadow Network has a strict set of rules that keep its members in check. These rules are supposed to prevent members from betraying each other. In practice, these rules mean you must be skilled enough to betray and take advantage of your fellow members without getting caught. Any who are found breaking these rules are immediately expelled from The Shadow network, and if you are not with them, you are against them.

The Races of Alaris
        The world of Alaris is home to many different creatures, sentient and non-sentient. In this section, I will give a brief overview of the sentient races, and explain their origins as well as their most common traits. Included with each of these races is a rarity tag, ranging from Common to Myth, which denotes how frequently these races can be found in the material plane. This is meant to be a very general category and describes the average for The Material Plane. Elves won’t be “Uncommon” in their capital city, but they have less numbers in the rest of the world than some other races. 
You might encounter a Common race frequently, usually having at minimum a moderate presence in all regions and settlements. Their passing is no matter of note. 
An Uncommon race is still seen frequently, although usually in smaller groups. You might find a few in large settlements, and few notice them in particular, unless they have other noteworthy traits or deeds. 
A Rare race is one seen only occasionally. You might find a group in a certain region, or a large settlement. They might earn a few curious glances from children or start a word or two of gossip when they pass through town.
A Very Rare race is one never seen often. It is not likely to find one in large cities and there may be only a few in a given region at a time. If they are in public, they are subjected to the curiosity of most everyone, and will give the local rumor mill plenty of excitement. Many folk have never seen a Very Rare race in their lives.
A Myth race is one so rare, several are unaware of their existence. The largest of cities may not hold any, and there may only be a small handful on The Material Plane. When a Myth race passes through, all eyes are on them, curious, scared, amazed, or suspicious.

Races of The Material Plane
Dragonspawn
Dragonspawn were created by the dragons who used their innate magical powers, as well as the blessing of The Allmother, to give life to minions of their own design. Dragonborn were the first creation, followed by Kobolds.
________________
Dragonborn
(Rare)
Dragonborn, the first creations of the dragons, were given life halfway through The First Era (around 2,000 years in), when dragons began to desire servants to work for them. Dragonborn were created to act as simple servants and warriors: living, serving, and dying at the behest of their masters. The dragons had made a mistake, however, and gave Dragonborn a small piece of their personality and power. This caused strife between master and servant. Eventually, one by one, Dragonborn revolted and threw off their masters. By The Era of Prosperity (around 4,000 years in) not a single dragonborn bowed to their draconic creators. Some dragons were enraged while others accepted their independence as a race. All agreed, however, that they would create no more Dragonborn, instead focusing their efforts on a new creation…
Dragonborn can trace their scale color directly back to their draconic progenitors, as well as a Dragonborn’s demeanor. Red Dragonborn are usually fiery and hot-headed, while Gold Dragonborn are typically more wizened and aloof. Since breaking free of the dragons, Dragonborn have mixed attitudes towards them. Many spite them for the enslavement of their race, yet some honor them as their creators. Most Dragonborn, considering the dragons who decided to create them are all long-dead, are content to simply avoid dragons (A good idea in general).
Most dragonborn range from 6-7 feet and typically weigh anywhere from 180-250 pounds. Their scale color is passed down from their parents, inheriting the scales of their mother or their father. Dragonborn typically live around 100 years, and reach adulthood around 20 years old.
________________
Kobolds
(Very Rare)
Kobolds were the second creation of the dragons, given life during The Era of Prosperity (around 4,500 years in). Dragons had learned from their previous error, and created a much more loyal and docile race. Kobolds were the result of this “servant race” and the dragons were well pleased with the result. To dragons, kobolds are the ideal servant. They treated their masters with intense reverence and respect, and were very industrious. They had shorter lifespans than Dragonborn and were physically weaker, meaning revolts were near-impossible. To this very day, many kobolds still serve their draconic masters.
Though kobolds primarily serve dragons, they take great pleasure in it. Many tribes serve a dragon for generations upon generations. When a tribe’s dragon dies, or is presumed dead, they leave and search for a new dragon. Subservience to dragons is actually not hard-coded into Kobolds, but it is the primary and most influential part of their culture. In actuality, Kobolds are naturally extremely dedicated, usually to a draconic master, but sometimes to other masters, goals, or even ideals. A rare few kobolds leave their homes and tribes and find something to dedicate themselves to. 
Kobolds tend to be short and have light builds, usually around 4 feet tall, and weighing 25 pounds on average. Their scales are always muddy brown, but their texture can range wildly from sleek, serpentine scales to jagged, rough scales. Kobolds usually live anywhere from 20 to 60 years, depending on the kindness of their draconic master. They reach adulthood around 4 years old.


________________
Dwarves
Dwarves were one of the prime races; the first humanoids created by The Divine. Dwarves were created as people of the earth, to make their homes in the mountain reaches and rocky hillsides. Dwarves are sturdy and reliable folk. The bonds of their clan, and of their honor, are sacred in their societies. As a whole, dwarves reach adulthood at 50, and can live up to 400 years
Hill Dwarf
(Uncommon)
Hill Dwarves are dwarves that live more nomadic lifestyles, originating from Dwarves that left the first Mountainhomes to populate distant peaks. Hill Dwarves are known as less serious and solemn than their mountain-dwelling cousins. They are traditionally very talented brewers, and most clans are welcomed to stay near a settlement, so long as they are willing to trade their brews.
Hill Dwarves usually reach heights of 4 ½ feet, and weigh around 125 pounds. They tend towards tan skin, and dark eyes, while their hair is usually a shade of red.
Mountain Dwarf
(Uncommon)
Mountain Dwarves are the descendents of the dwarves which lived in the first Mountain homes. Where they were digging ever deeper into the mountains, finding and working new metals. Mountain Dwarves have a strong military tradition and are known as some of the best warriors in Alaris. In their tunnels beneath the earth, they have also perfected a form of Rune Magic, inspired by the Auld magic of giants. Dwarven runesmiths are master enchanters and can create weapons of great power.
Mountain Dwarves are typically around 4 ½ feet tall, and weigh in at 150 pounds. They have dusky skin, bright, pale eye colors, and dark hair.
________________
Duergar
(Very Rare)
Duergar are the descendants of the most reclusive and secretive of the dwarves who experimented with Rune Magic in dangerous new ways. This magic frightened their fellow dwarves, so the duergar dug deep into the earth, eventually reaching the underdark. Over the generations, they became obsessed with secrecy, and they collapsed the tunnels which connected them to the Mountainhomes. They experimented with Rune magic, attempting to enchant themselves as opposed to weapons or objects. They succeeded in this endeavor, branding magical runes onto themselves, and obtaining magical abilities.
Generations of this magical technique, as well as the harsh environment of The Underdark, have left duergar significantly changed from their cousins nearer the surface. Being branded with magical runes is a coming-of-age ritual for duergar. Because of the drastic separation from their kin, the duergar’s sense of honor has been severely twisted. To a duergar, their word is their bond (they choose to interpret “their word” however it suits them best at the moment). A Duergar will use broad terms, or speak about promises in a roundabout way to avoid being tied down by their word. Those duergar who are not clever enough to spin their word to their favor, tend to remain silent altogether.
Duergar typically stand 4 ½ feet tall, and weigh around 150. Their skin is a faded gray, and their hair and eye color is cloudy white.
________________
Elves
Elves are elegant, lithe, and near-timeless beings that are known to love nature, magic, and all forms of the arts. To truly understand elves, one must know of their long lives and their magical past. Elves were created as one of the prime races by The Divine, to be creatures of the deep forests and star-touched glades. Elves were given an incredibly long life, which gives them a unique perspective on their existence. Many mortals see elves almost as alien creatures, due to their long lives and the aloof attitudes which often result from it. This separation is exacerbated by elves’ ability to trance, in which they simply meditate on their most recent memories, and give their bodies a short rest.
The long lives of elves would have made them slow to change and adapt, but an event known as The Great Diaspora, which occurred during The War of The Ancestors (around 1,000 years in) caused much change within the race. The Great Diaspora was brought about by an elven Highwizard named Yvellim who grew tired of the endless war. Using advanced magical techniques (many of which had not been fully understood until recently, and some of which are still a mystery), Yvellim constructed a powerful magical circle, nearly 100 feet in diameter. Using this magic circle, he transported all elves from The Material Plane to The Feywild, whether they were willing or not.
        
This event had catastrophic effects for the elves. While several were content with this new, peaceful plane full of wonders, many wanted to return to their homes in The Material Plane. It took a full year for the elves to open another portal to The Material Plane, and when they returned, they found much of their land had been taken during their absence. It took bitter and brutal fighting to reclaim their lands, the fruits of which can be found in their proud martial traditions which still exist to this day.
The effects of The Great Diaspora left a hint of fey magic in every elf, ensuring all current and future generations would hold a trace of The Feywild within them. This adds to their naturally long life, and most elves reach adulthood at 100 years old, and can reach ages of nearly 1,000.
High Elf
(Uncommon)
        High Elves are the more noble and refined among the elves (or uptight and haughty, depending on who you ask). They are the leaders and nobility of elvenkind and have extremely strong traditions of magical prowess and martial training. Most high elves hail from Ered-Thor, the capital of the elven lands, and the seat of The Heavenly Court.
        The Heavenly Court is the ruling body for the elven government, and is led by The Sun King and The Moon Queen. Many in the world consider Ered-Thor and The Heavenly Court to be the pinnacle of elegance and art. Here, the leaders of elvenkind work to defend their lands and ensure the wellbeing of their people.
        High Elves typically stand around 5 ½ feet tall, and usually weigh between 80 and 120 pounds. Their skin ranges from fair to tan, and their hair is usually either a shade of gold or silver, depending on their lineage. Their eye color tends to be bright blue or green.


________________
Wood Elf
(Rare)
        Wood Elves are those who choose to live alongside nature, nestled deep within the forests. Extremely stealthy and having a strong bond with the woods, wood elves are rarely seen unless they intend to be. The most perceptive might notice a rustling shadow overhead as these elves dash across the branches of their forests.
Wood Elves are very protective of their territory, and they fight off any who would infringe upon it. Most wood elves live together in small groups, patrolling and hunting their forests, striving to keep away outsiders. Their communities live among the trees, hidden from sight. They are typically secretive and severe.
Wood Elves are shorter than high-elves, usually about 5 feet tall and weighing 80 pounds on average. Their skin tone is usually coppery, and their eyes tend toward dark shades of green and brown.
________________
Drow
(Very Rare)
        Drow are the residents of the underdark and its grim caverns. During The Great War of The Ancestors, while trying to reclaim the elves' land, they used their magical expertise to develop a powerful weapon, powered by the souls of the fallen. They then used this weapon to devastating effect. The results were undeniably effective, but the weapon was considered an abomination by their peers. The ancestors of the drow refused to destroy their weapon, and fled to the underdark so as to escape the wrath of their kin.
Escaping to the underdark, drow spent generations developing their dark magics. Their life in the underdark led them to adapt, and they developed new appearances along with a strong form of darkvision, while the magic they practiced sowed malice in their hearts. To this day, the subterranean cities of the drow stand, where the most powerful and cruel rule. Few drow leave the underdark, but some few who have good hearts may leave to escape their vile kin.
Drow are typically 5 ½ feet tall, and usually weigh around 110 pounds. Their skin is a deep ebony, while their hair is stark white. Their eyes are usually a shade of red.
________________
Goblinoids
        Members of the “Goblinoids” group may seem to have little in common physically. During The Era of Prosperity (around 8,000 years in), The Divine Aspects of Darkness, War, and Death leveraged their powers against The Aspect of Life, forcing her to breathe life into their creations. Each created a race, and controlled them as near-mindless pawns. These creatures, unwillingly created by The Allmother, caused much evil in the world. When The Godspire was banished in the year 8988, Goblinoids gained their sentience and independence, though the mark of The Evil Aspects are still heavily present in their culture. Because of their history, Goblinoids are especially resistant to charms and mind-altering magic.
Goblin
(Uncommon)
        Goblins were created by The Reaper, Aspect of Death. Due to her malign influence, goblins thrive off of death. They are cruel and vicious creatures, treating life as a cheap thing. To goblins, the ability to kill, or have others kill for you, is the only right to rule. They are most naturally found in tribes, where their numbers are kept down by constant infighting. Goblin tribes will often raid nearby roads and settlements, stealing and killing as they please.
        Most of the violence that many think is inherent in goblins, is simply a result of their culture. When they were still under the hand of The Reaper, she ingrained a deep sense of satisfaction for killing. Goblins are creatures that find true purpose in bringing death. That is not to say a goblin cannot find purpose in other things, or turn their sights only on killing those who deserve it, yet few choose this path.
        Goblins usually stand 3 ½ feet tall, and weigh around 35 pounds. Their skin tones are typically greenish, and their eyes are usually yellow tones. The small amount of hair they have is usually deep black. Goblins reach adulthood at age 5, and (though highly uncommon) can reach ages of 30 years old.
Bugbear
(Rare)
Bugbears were the spawn of Shadowhand, Aspect of Darkness. They created bugbears to serve as spies, assassins, and thieves. Bugbears are naturally quiet and opportunistic, a strange set of traits for such a large creature. They are usually solitary travelers, finding good hiding places (be that a settlement with a shady underground, or a dense forest with plenty of traffic), and waiting for a perfect opportunity.        
Sometimes, bugbears come to travel with a small group of other bugbears, or to rule at the head of a goblin tribe. These groupings with others never last long, as bugbears are constantly seeking opportunities to betray their fellows for an advantage. Usually, these groups only last a few months at most, for when a bugbear decides to strike, the conflict always ends quickly. Bugbears have a natural inclination towards stealth and seeking opportunity, but are not inherently evil. Though it is a difficult challenge, some can learn to trust others, or at least not betray them first.
Bugbears usually stand anywhere from 6 to 8 feet tall, and usually weigh around 175 pounds. Their fur is usually a shade of brown, and their eyes are typically yellow. They reach adulthood around 10, and can live to be 70 at the oldest.
________________
Hobgoblin
(Rare)
Hobgoblins were the invention of The Father of Battle, Aspect of War. When he created Hobgoblins, he made those he would consider to be the perfect soldiers. Disciplined, organized, and united. Had He not been weakened by the banishing of The Godspire, The Father of Battle likely would have brought all other races to kneel beneath his Hobgoblin armies. Hobgoblins are a stark contrast to the other goblinoids, not seeking to betray their comrades. In fact, hobgoblins have extremely strong emotional bonds with their families, making them fight all the harder to protect them in battle. Hobgoblins have an incredible sense of near-magical loyalty and courage. Cowardice and backstabbing is all-but-unheard of in their ranks.
After being freed from The Father of Battle, hobgoblins formed into small armies. To hobgoblins, an army is their family. All hobgoblins treat warfare as a sacred art, and every last one of them consider fighting in a battle to be a great honor. Due to the influence of The Father of Battle, hobgoblins are creatures that feel great dissatisfaction during times of peace. This tends to lead hobgoblin armies towards near-constant warfare.
Hobgoblins were not created to be good or evil, simply to fight. Though they have free will now, the long-standing military traditions still urge most hobgoblins towards war and battle.
Hobgoblins are around 6 foot tall on average, and weigh in the 150-200 pounds range. Their skin tone is a shade of red, and they usually have black hair. Their eyes are always the same shade of brown. They reach adulthood around 20, and can live up to 60 years old.

Gnomes
Gnomes were the creation of The Allmother, Aspect of Life, and The Master of Aether, Aspect of Magic during The First Era (around 3,500 years in). The small creatures were created with a mind for tinkering, experimenting, and progressing technology. Gnomes are naturally very creative and inventive people. Many suspect that The Divine Aspects created gnomes to advance the civilization of Alaris, and bring an end to The Great War of Ancestors. Gnomes are usually around 2 ½ feet tall, and usually live around 300 years, while they reach adulthood around 25.
Forest Gnome
(Rare)
Forest Gnomes make their homes in forests, sometimes living among wood elves. They possess an inherent quickness and knack for stealth, and are able to communicate with small beasts. They tend to make their homes in the trees, oftentimes connecting several large trees together with a series of rope bridges, ziplines, and pulley networks. Forest Gnomes live in harmony with other tree-dwelling creatures. They prefer to remain hidden from outsiders and predators, and have developed an innate magic for simple illusions, which they use to protect themselves.
Forest gnomes often have tan skin tones, and usually weigh around 25 pounds. They have brown shades of hair, and their eyes are usually bright green or brown.

Rock Gnome
(Uncommon)
Rock Gnomes tend to make their dwelling in tunnels and along cliff sides. They are more eccentric and outgoing than their tree-dwelling cousins, and trade with outside settlements frequently. Rock Gnomes possess a near-reckless sense of invention, and several innovations in Alaris are attributed to them. Many rock gnomes take residence in The Tower of Wonders, working on exciting new innovations in every field.
Rock gnomes usually have fair or coppery skin tones, and typically weigh about 40 pounds. They usually have brown or blonde hair. Their eyes are usually a steely gray or light blue.
Svirfneblin
(Very Rare)
        Svirfneblin, or Deep Gnomes, are the descendants of hard-working gnomes who carved tunnels deep into the earth, eventually reaching the underdark. The living conditions in the lightless tunnels of the underdark has caused the gnomes to change, gaining a form of innate magic that helped them hide from the vile drow and duergar who wished to enslave them. Hard, honest work is a prime tenet of svirfneblin culture, and a colony will do anything to ensure they and their kin survive. 
Svirfneblin value precious gemstones, and find great satisfaction in mining and cutting them. Most svirfneblin colonies collect these rare gemstones in a single location, called a trove. A great trove is a mark of pride for a colony, and they guard it zealously. On very rare occasions or times of need, a svirfneblin colony may trade a few of their beautifully carved gemstones to outsiders or surface dwellers, in exchange for goods they cannot acquire elsewhere.
Svifneblin have skin tones ranging from gray to light purple, and usually weigh about 30 pounds. They have very little hair, but it is often stark white. Their eyes are often a blend of brilliant colors. Heterochromia is very common for svirfneblin.
        
Halflings
Halflings are friendly folk, created by The Allmother and The Illuminator, Aspects of Light and Life during The First Era (around 2,500 years in), for the simple purpose of being good folk. In a time where war was waged across all of Alaris, a folk who aimed to live a good, honest life were a strong example to other folk. Halflings fulfilled this purpose, creating communities that did not care for violence and that could forgive and forget grudges easily.
As halflings became more prolific in the world, folk of other races took notice, and some joined these quiet, happy communities. Halflings have free will, of course, and so some are bound to take to warfare, adventure, or unscrupulous lives. Most prefer, however, pleasant, quiet lives lived amongst good friends and good food. Who wouldn’t? Most halflings stand 3 ½ feet tall, and usually live 200 years, reaching adulthood at age 25.
Lightfoot Halfling
(Common)
Lightfoot Halflings are those that have a multigenerational history of living among others. They have a talent for speech, which they often use to de-escalate situations. Lightfoot Halflings also are skilled at moving through a crowd, and can obscure themselves in one to escape from any danger which cannot be stilled with words. Most lightfoot halflings make excellent diplomats and negotiators, and many other races are glad to reside among them.
Lightfoot halflings usually weigh around 50 pounds. They usually have tan skin and light shades of hair. Their eyes are often blue, green, or brown.

Stout Halfling
(Common)
Stout Halflings have lived in dwarven lands for several generations, and have adapted as such. Stout Halflings often take up residence with the nomadic hill dwarves, and are more used to the hardships of travel. They have adapted a strong metabolism, and have an innate resistance to poison.
Stout halflings tend to weigh around 70 pounds, and often have bronze skin tones. Their hair is usually a shade of red, and their eyes are usually a dark shade of green or brown.
Ghostwise Halfling
(Rare)
The ancestors of ghostwise halflings took up residence in the inhospitable Odorr Marshes, and adapted to it as time passed. The marshes are crawling with dangerous predators, explosive natural gasses, and deadly patches of quicksand. To survive this dangerous terrain, ghostwise halflings have developed a way to communicate to each other telepathically, keeping quiet while being able to warn their fellows of danger. Ghostwise villages are often eerily silent, and visitors never feel comfortable staying long.
Ghostwise halflings usually weigh around 60 pounds. They usually have pale skin tones, and their hair is usually stark black, sometimes with streaks of white. Their eye color is usually a shade of purple.

Half-Elves
(Rare)
Half-Elves are a rare sight in Alaris, as humans and elves rarely get along. There are always exceptions, however, and from them, half-elves are born. Half-Elves have a very hard time fitting in with either elves or humans, and often tend to have a strong sense of wanderlust. Many half-elves wander Alaris looking for adventure, and others simply seek a place to belong. Half-Elves tend to inherit a few traits of their elven parent, but most are scorned in elven society, or treated as second-class citizens.
Half-Elves usually live around 120 years, and reach adulthood around age 20. They often have a combination of traits from both of their parents.
Half-Orcs
(Rare)
Half-Orcs are typically in a very similar societal situation as half-elves, although orc tribes tend to be more egalitarian, and accept half-orcs as part of their tribe. Because of this, most half-orcs live with their orcish tribes. Very few remain within human society, and those that do are often treated poorly, or feared, by their fellows. Their orcish blood can lend them bursts of powerful endurance and strength.
Half-Orcs tend to live around 90 years, and reach adulthood around age 20. They usually have a combination of traits from both parents.

Humans
Humans are the most ubiquitous race in Alaris, managing to establish settlements in nearly every corner of the world. Humans are well known for their ambition and perseverance. Humans have a much shorter lifespan than most other races, and some speculate this is the reason behind their (reckless according to the more long-lived races) ambitions. Human demeanor is very diverse. Some may be brash, others calm. Many strive for honor, riches, or power, and others are content with simple pleasures. Some are good, some evil. Humans have spread across Alaris, and are now some of the most diverse creatures. Their essence is particularly open to the influence of other planes, meaning several other races were once humans who adapted to the magic of another plane.
Humans, just like in real life, can have any combination of skin tones, eye and hair colors, and other traits. Most humans reach adulthood around age 20, and they can live around 80 years.

Lizardfolk
(Rare)
        Lizardfolk are the cold-blooded reptiles that primarily reside in The Odorr Marshes. Food was scarce for these creatures, and the lizardfolk, ruthlessly practical, hunted the other sentient swamp creatures to extinction. Having secured themselves as the masters of The Odorr Marshes, the lizardfolk began to populate the marshes, draining it of most all natural materials. The civilization of the lizardfolk collapsed, and tribes fought over the few remaining resources. Today, lizardfolk tribes still fight over territory and supplies, and are remarkably practical with their resources.
        Lizardfolk live to a max of 50 years, and reach adulthood around age 5. Their scales can range in hue from green to red, and their eyes are always green. They have no hair, but they do grow frills along the back of their head and appendages, which are usually red, blue, or yellow.

Orcs
(Uncommon)
Orcs were one of the prime races, created by The Divine along with Alaris itself. They are very adaptable and resilient, and spread to the far corners of the world. Orcs were near-entirely aloof from The Great War of The Ancestors, and tended to keep to themselves in their small communities. Because of this, the society of orcs progressed slowly, not needing to make rapid advancements in technology. Their tribal society was peaceful, and rarely interacted with creatures of other races. When the 3-fold pact was signed by the humans, elves, and dwarves, they needed a common purpose to unify them, and some schemers thought the orcs would serve that purpose.
The members of The 3-fold pact turned their attention to the orcs and their lands, which the orcs defended with all their might. Their technology was outclassed, however, and the weight of three nations proved too strong to resist. The orcs were scattered far, to the most inhospitable and undesirable lands. Orc tribes still exist in these areas, and orcs tell stories of their ancestral lands, which they swear they will one day take back.
Orcs can live to a max of 100 years, and reach adulthood at age 20. Their skin tone can be green to teal, and can vary widely in brightness depending on their climate. Most orcs have yellow, amber, or brown eyes. Orc hair is typically always black.

Yuan-ti
(Very Rare)
Yuan-ti are the result of humans who were cursed by Shadowhand, Aspect of Darkness. During The Era of Prosperity (around 5,750 years in) a group of humans ventured into The Odorr Marshes, and faced great adversity fighting against the ruthless inhabitants of the swamps. Seeking divine aid to survive, they turned to Shadowhand and asked them to give the group a blessing, so that they might survive in the swamps. 
Shadowhand obliged, and made the humans into Yuan-ti, giving them serpentine adaptations which helped them survive. Crafty as ever, Shadowhand took something in exchange for the boon, their compassion. This left them as a cruel and crafty race. 
Other humans despised Yuan-ti, as did the other creatures of the swamp. And so they made their homes in secret lairs beneath the muck, plotting and scheming. When The Godspire was banished, Shadowhand could no longer withhold their compassion from them, although their culture has long held ruthlessness as the greatest virtue. To this day, they still live in their ancient temples beneath the marshes, crafting dastardly plots, though some choose to leave, and seek peace, or even adventure.
Yuan-ti can live up to 150 years, and become adults at age 20. Their skin covers a variety of human tones, but they always have faint patches of  scales on their body, often on their cheeks, arms, legs, and back. These scales are typically green or black. Their pupils are serpentine and their eyes are usually green or blue.

Fey Races
Changeling
(Myth)
Changelings are the result of humans who became trapped in The Shadowfell, and refused to serve The Raven Queen. In turn she cursed them for their insolence, leeching away their identities and appearances. Over generations, changelings adapted to these losses and became able to change and shift their bodies at-will, creating for themselves whichever identity they wished or related with. When The Godspire was banished, the sudden change of power in the planes caused all changelings to be shunted back to The Material Plane, where they remain to this day. Most live in communities of fellow changelings, traveling and taking forms as they please. Yet others choose to adopt a single persona and form, and fully rejoin society.
Changeling life cycles are strange. They become adults at age 20, but they have no definite maximum age. If a changeling adopts a permanent identity and rejoins society, they have a life expectancy equal to the race they choose to adopt. Not much is known about how or why adopting permanent identities can affect changelings. They are human-like in natural size and weight, although they can change both of those factors at-will. In their natural state, they have white skin, dark eyes, and white hair.

(Myth)

Kenku
(Very Rare)
        Kenku are an enigmatic race of raven-like creatures, which are thought to originate from The Shadowfell. As time passed, The Raven Queen grew curious about events on the other planes. Magic In Alaris
        This section is meant to describe what magic is in Alaris, and how it works. This covers how different types of spellcasting classes use magic in Alaris, and gives details as to the mechanics characters use when casting spells. This section is also meant to show the difference between divine and arcane magic. It’s always been important to me that magic makes sense in my homebrew world. I want people to be able to understand magic in Alaris to a level where they can see something happen and come to a conclusion on their own about how that just happened. If I had to rank it, I would say Alaris has a “hard” magic system. (Also, this section was originally written with a far more casual writing style, which I decided to leave in. Hope you enjoy it!)
________________
Intro To Magic
Mechanically, magic in Alaris is near-identical to magic in most high fantasy settings. Wizards use their arcane knowledge to cast powerful spells, Bards weave magic and music in harmony. Warlocks receive their eldritch powers from their patrons, Druids call upon nature itself. Clerics and Paladins look to their deities for their supernatural abilities. All classes use magic as you would expect.
        Thematically, however, magic users in Alaris have a few noticeable differences. I want to preface these notes with a disclaimer: Everything here is a suggestion, don’t feel like you have to stick along with the descriptions provided if it interferes with your character fantasy and theme. This is how it will look when NPCs use magic, so if you desperately desire continuity (as I do), make your magic appear in a similar way. Of course, there can be exceptions to these methods, and oftentimes they are found in PCs. Perhaps your character has found a different way to bring about magic. That’s great! Just be sure to have a reason for doing so, otherwise, stick with how magic is described in this section.
________________
What is Magic?
        We’ll answer that in a moment. First, we need to understand what fuels magic: Aether and Divine Power. Let’s start with Aether. Aether is a unique type of radiation in Alaris, which is bathed onto (and even into) the planet. Aether comes from a distant star and is reflected off of The Mage Moon, a celestial body in orbit around Alaris. This radiation is useless by itself, but a strange quality of The Mage Moon changes the radiation into a powerful form of energy. 
This energy must be used by casters in order to create magic. Certain moon phases can increase or decrease the levels of Aether in Alaris, which in turn affects the abilities of magic-users (For example, on a full moon, more Aether is being radiated to Alaris, thus magic users can pull more readily on it. During a new moon, very little Aether is reflected to Alaris, and so magic practitioners have a harder time gathering it for their uses).
Divine Power is the energy of The Divine Aspects; immensely potent and loaded with intent. Each Divine Aspect has an equal amount of Divine Power, which it can use at its discretion. As The Divine Aspects are locked away, their ability to directly influence the world is limited, and they tend to lend their power to their followers (Clerics and Paladins). Divine Power cannot be used by just any living being as Aether can. It has an intent along with it, which will only allow it to be used in a way that aligns with the will of its Divine Aspect. (As an example, Divine Power from The Aspect of Life cannot be used to fuel a spell that murders orphans). Only those very experienced with Divine Power can override its intent.
Now, the answer to the all-important question: “What is Magic?” In Alaris, magic is the manipulation of Aether or Divine Power to produce a specific effect. Examples are plentiful: a wizard creating a destructive blast of fire or a cleric healing a wound. The effect created doesn’t matter; if it is fueled by Aether/Divine Power and does something, then it’s magic.
________________


But how do you use magic? It isn’t (usually) as simple as willing something to happen. In order to use magic, you must gather Aether into yourself, and then guide the Aether into performing an effect. Usually these effects are collectively called Spells, but can also be infused into an item, in which case it is called Enchanting (not to be confused with the infuriatingly named school of Enchantment, which definitely should have been named the school of Glamour, but I digress).
Ok, so you must gather Aether into yourself, then guide it into an effect. Let’s break that down into two steps: Gathering and Casting, and start by explaining the former. Aether is invisible and intangible to the naked eye, although it is always present. You can’t gather it in a basket or a breath so you must use your Spark. Every living being has a Spark, though some are stronger than others. You can think of a Spark like a pair of magical lungs. It Gathers Aether into yourself and then Casts it out of you back into the world. Each and every spark is unique, and often reflects the personality of the individual. A hot-tempered person may have a fiery red spark that pulsates and flashes with energy, while a serene individual could have a cool blue spark that flows and shifts in shape. No two Sparks are the same, and the differences in them can cause changes in how certain spells work (but not the result, which means it’s all flavor, baby).
Like any other muscle, a Spark can be trained, although the process is much more arduous than a typical leg day. Through long periods of use, a Spark can grow in strength (explaining why level-20 magic users, who have been using magic for a longer time, have more spell slots than your puny level-1 self). Using up all of your spell slots means your Spark is fatigued, and you are unable to Gather any significant amount of Aether. When channeling powerful amounts of Aether, it is not uncommon for the magic-user’s eyes to glow.
That explains how Gathering works, but Casting is (usually) more than simply breathing out. If we are to stick with the magical lungs metaphor that is quickly becoming unwieldy, Casting is akin to breathing exercises. Certain techniques which oftentimes take practice, produce a certain desired effect. Each and every class has a different method of Casting, so I will detail them here:


Wizards
        Wizards make use of Arcane magic, and are the most common form of magic-users in Alaris. Wizards Gather Aether like everyone else, and then make use of components: specific words, gestures and materials to Cast. These components are immensely complicated and difficult to perform, and thus take enormous amounts of study and practice. Anyone can become a wizard with enough time, practice, and resources, although few have the luxury or the motive to do so. Wizards with stronger Sparks tend to have more initial success in the craft, and are thus more likely to be chosen as an apprentice or be given the resources to learn wizardry.
        A wizard's components are the keys to Casting. Verbal components are words of power, spoken in the very language of Aether itself. Verbal components don’t simply sound like any other words, even an untrained ear can hear the power laced within. Somatic components are Runes of power, written on the canvas of reality itself. Oftentimes these Runes are drawn in the air and glow faintly. Material components are substances which help to focus and guide the spell. Most often, this is in the form of an Arcane Focus, but in a pinch, wizards can also use specific materials for different spells to achieve the same result.
        Wizardry is treated as a form of science in Alaris, and an identical combination of components will produce an identical result every time. Most wizards tend to fine-tune their spells to work best with their Spark, creating different methods to achieve a similar end result, and often reflect their personality.


________________
Sorcerers
        Sorcerers are the ones responsible for every instance of  “(usually)” in this doc. They connect to magic in a far more personal way than other magic-users. Their Sparks are connected closely with the Aether in the world (usually in ways determined by their subclass). This innate connection to magic allows them to call upon it in a much simpler way than most other casters. Sorcerers discover, through trial-and-error or simply through an innate sense, how to channel Aether into spells.
Sorcerers still need to follow the rules for components, performing somatic gestures and speaking words laced with power (These words can be anything, not only specific incantations. Regardless of what is said, the power within the words is obviously magical). Sorcerers never require material components for their spells. Sorcerers’ unique connection to Aether also allows them to shape and manipulate aspects of their spells on-the-fly, using their metamagic options.


________________
Bards
        Bards are a very unique spellcasting class, drawing their magic only in part from Aether. Bards get their magical abilities from knowledge of The Song of Creation, a powerful song, the words of which are known to few, and fewer understand it enough to draw power from it. The Song of Creation carries in its melodies a mix of arcane and divine magic, which allows Bards to manipulate both Aether and Divine Power, weaving them fluently into their spells.
        The Song of Creation is said to have existed since the beginning of time. It is an integral part of reality itself, and both Divine Power and Aether respond to its rhythms. Bards, for some reason or other, have the ability to understand this song and use its power to cast spells. Bards don’t store the song in their minds and can’t simply teach it to others. The Song of Creation is kept within a Bard’s Spark, and they can learn to use it with an innate understanding. Bards only need to understand a few notes of The Song of Creation to make use of its power, but the more they know of the song, the stronger their magical abilities become. By traveling and meeting other folk, Bards can learn more pieces to the song, increasing their spellcasting potential. 
        Through incorporating The Song of Creation into their words and music, bards can use musical instruments as arcane foci, channeling The Song of Creation through them. Verbal components are much more fluid and can be anything; a verse to a song, a quip, or a simple humming melody. Somatic components are usually simple gestures and flourishes.


________________
Clerics
        Clerics are magic-users that have been chosen by a Divine Aspect. Since each Divine Aspect is limited in their power, Clerics are very rare, often chosen from among the most devoted followers of an Aspect.  A Cleric’s magical power comes from their Divine Aspect, and they do not typically draw their power from Aether. Instead, their powers come directly from their Aspect into their Spark, called upon whenever it is needed. This connection causes the spark of a Cleric to be fundamentally altered to contain their Aspect’s Divine Power, making it noticeably different when observed.
        Because of their unique form of divine magic, Clerics come to exhibit the qualities of their Aspects over time, in their magic and their appearance. Given time, a cleric of The Divine Aspect of War will grow threatening in appearance, forming scars where they have not been wounded, and channeling magic in a violent way. In a similar way, a cleric of Darkness may exude an uneasy feeling, seep more deeply into the shadows, and lie with ease. The longer a cleric serves their aspect, the more pronounced the effects are.
        Clerics can also pull more heavily on their Divine Aspect, channeling their divinity (using their Channel Divinity feature, in case I lost you) in short bursts. This is taxing on the cleric’s Spark, and can only be used rarely. Divine Power has an intent, and though a Cleric can be filled with this power, it is more difficult to use it, especially for beginners. To make using their powers easier, a Cleric can use a holy symbol of their Aspect as a divine focus, and their somatic and verbal components are typically prayers, holy gestures, or related to their Aspect or their cause.


________________
Paladins
        Very similar to Clerics, Paladins also get their magic from a Divine Aspect, although theirs is obtained in a different way. While Clerics are simply chosen for their powers by The Divine Aspects themselves, Paladin swear a sacred oath, not only to a Divine Aspect, but oftentimes to a purpose or a cause of their own. This oath, when spoken with reverence and faith, and after being accepted by The Divine Aspects and the paladin, becomes a binding agreement, immalleable by either party, and a conduit for Divine Power.
        This Sacred Oath is given power by The Divine Aspects who support the tenets sworn. For example, if a Paladin takes the Oath of The Ancients, and swears to live a good life and lead others to happiness, then The Divine Aspects of Life, Light and Peace might deign to give some of their Divine Power to The Sacred Oath. The Oath then grants the power to the paladin. The Oath protects the paladin’s Spark from being altered, and as the paladin devotes themselves more fully to their Oath, the power they are granted grows.
        Because of the influence of The Sacred Oath, a paladin carries their Divine Power not in them, but around them (This is represented by their Aura of Protection, Courage, and whatever Aura they gain from their subclass.), allowing them to share the benefits of Divine Power with their party.
        Should a Paladin ever break their Sacred Oath, they no longer gain any power from it. Paladins of enough experience using Divine Power can commandeer their oath, fouling (yet retaining) the Divine Power it contains, and becoming an Oathbreaker. This is extremely rare, as most Divine Aspects do not power the Sacred Oath of a paladin that lacks true conviction.


________________
Warlocks
        Back to arcane casters now, with my favorite: The Warlock. Warlocks gain access to Aether through their patrons, in a special kind of magic called Pact Magic. A Warlock’s Pact is similar to how a Cleric gains their Divine Power, the methods having been discovered by creatures of great power. A Warlock’s Spark is bound to their patron’s, and they use this connection to siphon Aether and magical knowledge from their patron. This allows them to use their Pact Magic. Pact magic is almost always different in shape and form from other magic. The power and knowledge to cast spells has been granted by a powerful patron, with plenty of Aether to spare. 
These patrons have discovered secrets of magic and have unique ways to use magic and spells. This means that verbal and somatic components are often dependent on the nature of The Patron (A warlock of the fiend may speak their verbal components in Infernal, and a Fey Warlock may use expressions of emotion as a somatic component). Typically, a patron also grants their warlock an Arcane Focus, which further cements their pact.
This unique knowledge of magic also manifests as a warlock’s Eldritch Invocations, ways to use magic that are all but unknown, granted to you by your patron. Unlike paladins, who must follow a Sacred Oath, a warlock’s powers are firmly placed in the hands of their patron, and can be given or taken away as seen fit. Methods which a warlock could use to break their pact exist, but are near-unknown.


________________
Artificers
        Artificers function very similarly to Wizards when it comes to using Aether. Through intensive training and studying, an artificer can create special devices using runes, inscriptions, and magical materials, to channel Aether and produce a desired result. Artificer spells are usually represented as magic devices which produce a spell-like result. (Firebolt may be an enchanted pebble launched by a magical slingshot. Spider Climb may be a set of magical climbing spikes, etc)
        An Artificer must Gather Aether themselves, and then use it to power these arcane devices (Letting their creations do the Casting). An Artificer can use their extensive knowledge of magical devices to create new ones, and have a degree of flexibility over wizards, being able to swap out devices (spells) over a long rest. An Artificer’s knowledge of arcane tinkering also provides them the ability to create magic items, which can be given to their party or used themselves.
        An Artificer uses similar components to a wizard, although in a different manner. Verbal components are spoken command words, which activate their magical devices. Somatic components are often simply aiming the device, or manually activating the device. Artificers can use a set of craftsman tools as an arcane focus in place of material components, using them to add some last-minute adjustments to their devices.


________________
Druids
        Druids are characterized by their deep connection to nature and the world itself. Through a deep understanding and communion with nature, Druids learn to call upon the Aether of the world. The same power that falls upon the forest at night and suffuses the stones is granted to Druids. Druids Gather in a similar way as other arcane casters, but the Aether that they wield is from nature, the world itself, and nature responds to it more readily.
        Druids are also able to use this deep bond they have with nature to take on the shape of beasts and other creatures that call the world of Alaris home. Using the Aether given to them by nature, Druids can temporarily take on the form of beasts which they have seen before. In addition to this boon, Druids can speak Druidic, the language of Nature itself. This language cannot be taught, only learned by those with a careful ear and deep respect for nature, making it unique.


Rangers
        Rangers are very similar to Druids in where they get their magic from, although they use their connection with Nature in a different way. Where Druids can use their natural understanding to create more magical effects, Rangers use it to become masters of their terrain. Through a ranger’s communion with Nature, they understand the ins and outs of a certain type of terrain, and use the knowledge to their advantage. They combine their gifts from Nature with their martial prowess to devastating effect, making them terrifyingly effective hunters.

Quasi-Magic
        “Quasi” Is a fun adjective to use, right? There are a few powers in Alaris which may appear to be “magic” but are actually outside the definition, as they have nothing to do with Aether or Divine Magic. There are three forms of Quasi-Magic in Alaris: Psionics, Ki, and Aulder Magic. Let’s dive right in and explain them!
Psionics
        Psionics is the label for any kind of psychic powers with tangible effects in Alaris. Examples might be lifting an object with Telekinesis, or reading the thoughts of a creature using Telepathy. Psionic power is the power of the mind. Every individual has Psionic potential, but it remains locked behind mental barriers. 
The “key” to this latent Psionic potential is different for each individual. For some, intense personal meditation is needed. For others, they must have another Psionic to help them unlock their powers. For some still, Psionic powers can only be unlocked through a traumatic event. The random nature of these “Psionic Keys” has led to Psionics being extremely rare within Alaris, with very few practitioners.
Those who unlock their Psionic potential may gain access to a simple form of power (such as the Telekinetic or Telepathic feats). Others may develop their powers further, learning to use their Psionic powers as swords and shields, propelling their bodies across battlefields (like a Psi Warrior Fighter). Some can learn to use their Psionics in a more subtle way, linking the minds of their allies, and assaulting their foes with mental blades (Looking at you, Soulknife Rogue).
________________
Ki
        Ki is an energy which can only be manipulated by Monks. Ki is found in every living body, and can be shaped, guided, and even stopped by a skilled practitioner. Ki has been called many things by many practitioners, and no one is truly certain what “Ki” is. The most commonly accepted explanation is that: “If Psionics are the powers of the mind, then Ki is the power of the soul.” 
        There are various ways to make use of Ki, and different techniques are known to different monks. Usually, this is tied to a specific monastery which teaches a monastic tradition. Each monastic tradition has a different view of what Ki is, and perhaps because of their beliefs, they are able to manipulate Ki in that way. 
As an example, Monks who follow The Way of The Four Elements believe that Ki is the energy of the elements, and can use their Ki to manipulate, summon, and control the elements themselves. Monks who follow The Way of The Astral Self believe that Ki is the power of your true self, and use their Ki to manifest pieces of their astral form.


________________
Aulder Magic
        Aulder Magic is the final type of magic, which I am, of course, going to break down into two parts: Draconic (for dragons) and Rune (for Giants). Dragons and Giants are the most ancient and powerful creatures in Alaris, having been the first creations of The Divine. These creatures have existed for so long that they have a kind of Quasi-Magic unique to their species. Let’s start with Dragons, because they are objectively cooler.
        Draconic Magic is innate to all dragonkind. They know how to use it from birth, although it grows stronger as they age. Draconic magic is the power that gives Dragons their breath weapons, changes the climate around their lairs, and grants them their Frightful Presence. It is thought that Draconic magic may also be the reason for their love of treasure.
        Some creatures may have access to Draconic Magic, and can use its power to great effect. Drakewarden Rangers, Draconic Bloodline Sorcerers, and Way of The Ascendent Dragon Monks all make use of Draconic Magic in different ways.
         Rune Magic is far more stable that other forms of magic. Ancient Giants were masters of the art of Runecarving, forming letters and characters which could shape the world around them. Runes are immensely powerful, yet take an extremely long time to create properly. An average Runecarver may only produce one to two runes every fifty years of work. The nature of runes require them to be exact, and even the slightest, imperceptible mistake can cause the rune to be inert. Once a rune is carved, however, there is little that one could do to stop it. Runes written on the sides of mountains could be dated back to the first years of Alaris, and still function to this day.
        Runes can be replicated to produce similar, yet less powerful results by mortals. Rune Knight Fighters channel Runic Magic, while Artificers and Dwarven smiths make use of Runes for magical creations. Though these Runes are more accessible to mortals, they pale in comparison to the power of true Giant-carved Runes.

Divine Aspects
Alaris was created by The Divine, and the aspects of the omnipotent being still influence the course of life today. From the tumult of The Shattering until present day, life in Alaris carries the fingerprint of its creator, in all of its myriad forms. Even locked away in The Godspire, The Divine Aspects still hunger to influence the world, each wishing to alter it to best suit their idea of perfection. Some pray to The Divine Aspects while others curse by them, and some are content to ignore them, but none can deny the monumental impact their actions have made, and the changes that they can and will bring about.
________________
History Of The Divine Aspects
        Formed from The Shattering in the year 792, The Divine Aspects are each singular, imperfect reflections of the various aspects of The Divine, the deity that created Alaris. When The Divine was Shattered, Their essence filled The Godspire, which was created by mortals to represent the aspects of creation they thought most relevant.
        Each Divine Aspect remained within The Godspire, though they could influence the world with their Divine Power. Each Divine Aspect had an exclusive view of the perfect world, however, and they each used their powers to move Alaris in that direction. The Allmother would perform miraculous healings, and The Reaper would introduce new plagues. The Tranquil Heart used their powers to end wars, while The Father of Battles ignited new ones. This pressure, each Divine Aspect attempt to push the world towards their perfect vision, ended up balancing them out. Instead of a world dominated by one vision, Alaris had pieces of all The Divine Aspect’s designs. Mortals could live, and would inevitably die. War would sweep the world, but eventually peace could return. There may be lawful cities and untamed wilderness, and times of light and dark. In short, Alaris became as it always had been.
When The Godspire was banished in the year 9012, and The Divine Aspects with it, the dynamic of powers were drastically changed. No longer could The Divine Aspects influence the world directly, they were confined to an unknown realm, only able to see pieces of the world, and only able to speak to mortals through vague visions and dreams. This change ended the era when The Divine Aspects could interfere with mortal lives with their own hands.
________________


In this new era, they now invested their power into mortals, having to simply hope and trust that the mortals would enact their will. Divine Power could be granted in small amounts to a mortal’s spark, which would allow them to wield it. When granted directly to a mortal in this way, it would slowly change them, gradually shifting their body and their mind to better align with The Divine Aspect that granted them the power. To go along with this, Divine Power comes with an intent, which prevents it from being used for purposes that are contradictory to its source’s wishes.
Many follow The Divine Aspects and are not granted any Divine Power, but they still benefit from their faith in small ways. Through unknown means, tiny amounts of Divine Power can leak through the realms, and coalesce in certain rituals and rites. Though small, the effects of these ceremonies do affect the world, meaning many mortals benefit from worshipping a Divine Aspect and partaking in prayers and observances. Many common folk pay their respects to most or all of The Divine Aspects, as their lives may involve several of Their domains. Sometimes mortals choose to follow one Aspect in particular, devoting themselves to carry out that Aspect's will in Alaris. When Divine Aspects invest their Divine Power into a mortal’s spark, they usually (though not always) make clerics out of their most faithful followers, as they are most likely to act in accordance with that aspect's will.

The Allmother: Divine Aspect of Life
Domain
        The Allmother’s touch can be seen all across Alaris. Holder of The Divine’s power to create life, The Allmother had a hand in the creation of nearly all of the races and creatures of Alaris after the shattering. Her Domain can be organized by a few ideals: Growth, Beginnings, and Health
        The Allmother loves that which flowers, blooms, and reproduces. Growth is blessed by her, and she delights in all stages of it throughout Alaris, both in creatures and other organisms. She presides over new births and comings-of-age.
        The Allmother pens the beginning of all things. From new life, to a new age, the creation of something new is adored by Her. To be created, and to fight to remain in the world is a holy act.
        Health is a virtue in The Allmother’s eyes. She blesses physicians and herbalists who seek to aid the sick and cure diseases and wounds. She is almost always against bringing harm to another creature, although she does (very reluctantly) allow violence in the circumstances where it will preserve more life than it takes.
Perspective
        In the eyes of The Allmother, life is sacred and should never be brought to an end. Death, decay, and sickness are antithetical to her worldview, and have no place in a perfect world. She strives, through her followers, to preserve and protect the lives, growth, and health of all living creatures.
        Her goal is a world in which all creatures live forever, and are always able to grow. A world in which plants cover every surface, and creatures continue to reproduce, and yet never die, living eternal lives of perfect health. Even if trivial things must be sacrificed to accomplish this, like quality of life or free will, The Allmother would gladly make such a trade if she were able.
Followers
The Allmother’s followers are mostly doctors or farmers. Many of The Heraldic Order worship Her, seeking to bring life and growth as they travel Alaris. Among these is an exclusive group known as The Cure. Its members are experts who study all types of medicines and illnesses, hoping to one day rid Alaris of all disease and sickness. The Cure is responsible for protecting civilization from many deadly plagues which could have proven catastrophic. If one cannot find treatment from a member of The Cure, then none likely exists.
It is accepted amongst her followers that, at times, violence is the only way to stop further violence. In this context, though distasteful, her followers are allowed to bring harm to others, but they are to act with extreme caution, using the minimum possible harm to stop a target.
________________
The Illuminator: Divine Aspect of Light
Domain
        During the day, The Illuminator’s hand upon Alaris is easiest to see. Heir of the sun and all light upon the world, The Illuminator is a benevolent figure that cares deeply for the mortal races. He concerns himself with Hope, Truth, and Luminescence.
        The sun is the bringer of day, the light of the morning. Under its bright glow, mortals can live their lives with joy and happiness. The sun's warmth and positivity is a constant reminder of hope: no matter how dark the night is, The Illuminator will always bring up the morning sun.
        Within light, things are seen fully as they are. The Illuminator holds the value of truth as highly important. To speak truth is to spread His light, and to clear away the darkness.
        Of course, most important to The Illuminator is the light itself. Luminescence, the state of giving off light, is sacred to The Illuminator. For that which is luminescent is a spreading truth and hope wherever it may be. 


Perspective
        The Illuminator wills that all creatures live ever in the light. Darkness, Despair, and Lies are all intolerable beneath the eyes of The Illuminator. He aims to spread his piercing light across all Alaris, banishing darkness and night forever.
A perfect world, in His mind, is one that is ever bright and hopeful. A place with no sadness or despair, where the blazing sun is ever in the sky, where none can utter a lie.
________________
Followers
Many followers of The Illuminator end up working for The Heraldic Order, where they spread his radiance throughout the world as they travel. His followers are called to speak only the truth, to keep and foster hope, and to always keep the darkness at bay. 
One of the highest positions that can be held by a follower
of The Illuminator is that of a lighthouse keeper. Across the coasts, and especially on The Sea of Spikes, several clerics of The Illuminator maintain and man lighthouses to guide ships safely through the dark, and rescue any that crash against the rocks.

The Judging Hand: Divine Aspect of Justice
Domain
        The Judging Hand is fair, and unbiased. Their greatest value is equality and order. The Judging Hand presides over those who uphold and exact just systems of law, and cares deeply for those who abide by them. The Judging Hand rules over Protection, Law, and Retribution
        Rules and order exist for the purpose of protection, being a kind of social contract that protects mortals from each other’s whims. In the eyes of The Judging Hand, this is a noble and just cause to fight for. To protect someone, especially those that cannot protect themselves, is a blessed pursuit in His eyes.
        The law is sacred, and of extreme importance to The Judging Hand. Without the law, society would collapse in on itself, and inequality would run rampant. To break the law is to invite disorder and suffering, while following the law is a noble and righteous pursuit.
        When one does break the law, they must be punished appropriately. For true equality means that all receive exactly what they deserve, without special treatment or exceptions. To dole out punishment, or submit yourself to just consequences, is a holy task to undertake according to The Judging Hand.


________________
Perspective
        The Judging Hand believes that a world of pure order would be paradise. A world where none would act against their agreements, or seek to take advantage of others is the goal of The Judging Hand. Those that impose just laws and order are paramount to society, as they maintain order, and fight off chaos.
        Unjust laws or systems, however, fall outside of The Judging Hand’s blessing. A system that perpetuates inequality knowingly, would violate its own purpose, and thus nullify itself. The Judging Hand wishes that all systems be perfect, and urges his followers to constantly assess their laws and rules, to ensure that corruption has not fouled them.
Followers
Many followers of The Judging Hand are a part of The Heraldic Order, and travel across Alaris, settling disputes, protecting those that need aid, upholding the rule of law, and exacting retribution on those that break it. Followers of The Judging Hand have extensive codes which they swear to, dictating the proper way to act in various scenarios.
These codes are held as integral to perpetuating justice in the world. As mortals are flawed, they trust not their own perspectives and biases, but instead they follow these codes, which are far closer to perfection than any individual could be. Many followers of The Judging Hand tattoo certain pieces of the code on their body, if they feel them to have a particular importance.

The Tranquil Heart: Divine Aspect of Peace
Domain
        Serene and deeply empathetic, The Tranquil Heart mandates peace and understanding in Alaris. In a world so fraught by conflict and chaos, The Tranquil Heart wishes to give rest and reprieve to the world. They preside over Empathy, Sacrifice, and Unity.
        To feel what another creature is feeling is integral to peace and understanding. To this end, The Tranquil Heart wishes that all creatures attempt to understand each other before acting. Creatures that cannot empathize, cannot be trusted to act in the interest of any but themselves.
        Acting in the interest of others, even to the extent of harming one’s self, is a holy and pure thing, according to The Tranquil Heart. To sacrifice yourself brings about peace, and helps those who are not enlightened enough to understand. 
        The goal of both empathy and sacrifice is unity; The state of togetherness in which many people act towards a common goal. To do this, one must set aside conflict and differences. Uniting with others is the most important thing a mortal can do in the eyes of The Tranquil Heart.


________________
Perspective
        Peace is a state of perfection in the eyes of The Tranquil Heart, a state which benefits all mortals that submit to it. Conflict, in any form, is accursed, and should be avoided at all costs. For conflict stems from differing opinions, and will result in some benefitting, and others being harmed. Wars, battles, contests, and arguments are all worthless at best, and destructive at worst.
        All great tragedies were brought about by conflict, which The Tranquil Heart detests. A world without conflict, then, is one in which all may live in harmony. This is Their goal, to bring all mortals into a state of unity, in which there are no differences of opinions, or conflicts to arise from them.
Followers
Like the other good-aligned Divine Aspects, The Tranquil Heart’s followers often join The Heraldic Order. Among their ranks, clerics are far more rare than followers of the other aspects, as The Tranquil Heart is extremely particular with which mortals are invested with Their Divine Power.
War-widows and wounded warriors are among the most devoted of Their followers, as they have seen the devastation that wars can bring firsthand. Similar to The Allmother’s followers, those that follow The Tranquil Heart detest conflict and violence, yet still engage in it as a lesser evil. There are a small number of followers that take it upon themselves to fight, so that the vast majority of their peers can exist in peace. These self-selected members are known as The Penitent.

The Essence of Nature: Divine Aspect of Elements
Domain
        Watching over the natural lands as they ever grow and change, The Essence of Nature is worshipped and feared by many who live amongst the edges of civilization. He is pragmatic and blunt, but not without care for those who respect His domain. The Essence of Nature is the ruler of Change, The Four Elements, and Wildness.
        All that exists came from something, and will someday become another. The Essence of Nature adores the process of changing, and cherishes watching the cycles of the natural world, from beginning to end, to a new beginning. Change is a valuable and necessary process that allows old things to become a part of something new.
        All of nature and the natural world comes from the four primal elements: fire, water, air, and earth. Each of these elements is tied to The Essence of Nature, who demands mortals respect them. For no forces of the world are as pure as the elements themselves.
        The refusal to bow to another's will is the heart of wildness, a virtue which The Essence of Nature promotes. Wild beasts are the pinnacle of this: refusing to take orders, choosing to die rather than to accept a master. Wildness is freedom, stubbornness, and an acceptance of the chaos of the world; an understanding that you can only attempt to control yourself.
________________
Perspective
        A perfect world, if described by The Essence of Nature, would be one of shifting wilderness which spanned the planes. A world in which no mortals attempted to control one another, and all lived by the strength of their own hand, not removed from the food chain as many wish to be. The cycle of life would be forever underway, never being stalled or its course shifted by ambitious mortals.
        His goal is to tear down that which is unnatural: towns and cities which only extract from the nearby environment, and do not introduce any benefit to the creatures which live upon it. In these places of civilization, mortals assert rules upon each other, allowing themselves to be guided along in life by other’s expectations. 
Followers
Followers of The Essence of Nature typically reside in the wilderness. Rarely do civilized folk encounter them. Few worship Him, but those who do are typically very pragmatic, and considered eccentric at best, or extremist at worst. Those that follow The Essence of Nature take it upon themselves to be the voice of the wild, and speak to other mortals on behalf of nature itself.
Many of His followers have good relationships with Druids and Rangers, often working alongside them when needed. As The Essence of Nature is connected very strongly to The Elemental Planes, those that worship him have established elemental temples across the planes.

The Patron of Labors: Divine Aspect of Trade
Domain
        Steadfast and hardworking, The Patron of Labors diligently watches over those that craft and trade. He is enterprising and ambitious, and supports his followers that act the same. The Patron of Labors claims as his domain Craftsmanship, Exchange, and Dedication.
        The Patron of Labors holds craftsmanship in high regard. The process of taking raw, unworked materials, and turning them into something better brings beauty and enrichment into the world. No matter the craft, be it a tool, garment, furniture, or art, it is seen as a holy work in His eyes.
        To have and hoard benefits one mortal little, but the process of trading benefits many, much. Exchange is a blessed pursuit, backed by The Patron of Labors. To trade what you possess, in exchange for that which you do not enriches both parties involved. Not only goods fall under His domain, but also exchange of culture and of information.
        Devoting one’s self to improvement and the furthering of one’s skills is a righteous and worthwhile pursuit in the estimation of The Patron of Labors. To work hard, spending time, effort, and skill on a project, then taking pride in the finished product is the divine act of dedication. Every mortal, like a tool, should strive to accomplish their own purpose to the fullest possible extent.
________________
Perspective
        The Patron of Labors sees the world as a place of crudeness, both in substance and in the mortals that inhabit it. A perfect world would be one in which all moved towards refinement. Mortals know exactly what they exist to do, and doing so to their best ability, working the materials of this world into a state of perfection.
        All would exchange gladly, trading and bargaining, not grasping at possessions, but all things to be bought and sold freely. Needless professions, those which do not add value to the world, like kings and soldiers, would cease, and all would focus instead on improving themselves and their environment.
Followers
In a notable departure from the typical follower of other Divine Aspects, few that follow The Patron of Labors do so full-time. Oftentimes, His priests and clerics are also merchants, who travel across the world, buying and selling goods.
Services from followers of The Patron of Labors are never free. They always ask for donations for their services. In fact, many take great offence when asked to work pro bono. Insisting that giving out charity means that their work has no value, as they receive no payment for it.

The Master of Aether: Divine Aspect of Magic
Domain
        Mystical and sage, The Master of Aether watches those that ply with the magical radiation known as Aether. She is enigmatic, and is very interested in the strange and otherworldly, though She would rarely speak on the matter. The Master of Aether manages the domains of Arcana, Education, and The Moon
        Those that observe and manipulate Aether are under the domain of The Master of Aether. Arcana is the study and mastery of arcane magic, learning how to harness the magical power that emanates from The Mage Moon. This aspiration to wield power that is greater than one’s self is admirable.
        To learn and to teach, the journey of education is sacred to The Master of Aether. Ignorance, though considered preferable to many mortals, is inexcusable. She smiles upon those that realize their lack of understanding, and seek to remedy it through learning. Curiosity, along with this, is highly regarded by Her.
        Through the night, The Mage Moon shines, piercing the darkness with its otherworldly blue light. It does not reflect light from the sun, but rather a strange distant star, and through reflecting its light, it changes it into a form which mortals can harness. The Master of Aether claims not the night sky, but the specks of otherworldly light which break up the unending void. She embraces the mystery of the unknown stars.
________________
Perspective
        Mortal lives are typically short, and often boring. The Master of Aether feels pity for these beings that spend so much of their lives simply fighting to exist. Aether is a tool that is far beyond the capacity of mortals, one that allows a single one to make extreme changes and accomplish great feats that are otherwise impossible. A force which elevates those that can use it. She cares not if Aether is used to create or destroy, only that it is used
        A perfect world is one in which all mortals can freely use Aether, and are in a constant state of learning and experimentation. All people using magic in all aspects of their lives, becoming better through it, and discovering the mysteries of the universe. A perfectly enlightened, wholly magical world is the aim of The Master of Aether.
Followers
The Master of Aether is often worshipped by sorcerers and wizards. Many teachers also follow Her doctrines. Clerics which can wield the Divine Power of The Master of Aether have very interesting abilities, being able to use Divine Power to command Aether.
Stories say that the legendary Order of The Crystal Moon, the group which banished The Godspire, were once followers of The Master of Aether. Many of her followers today search Alaris for hidden knowledge and information, hoping to discover the truth behind these ancient historical events.

Shadowhand: Divine Aspect of Darkness
Domain
        Ever watching and waiting in the dark recesses of Alaris, Shadowhand is a cunning and ruthless divine aspect that claims darkness as their territory. They bless opportunists that lie, cheat, or steal to get what they want. Shadowhand’s domains are Deception, Opportunism, and Gloom.
        Truth is vulnerability and weakness. Deception, then, is strength. To manipulate others into thinking, acting, and believing in ways which you wish is a virtue and power. To craft an image or persona that others take as true gives you influence over those you have deceived.
        To wait, to watch, and to strike at the perfect moment is a thing of beauty. Opportunism, whether stabbing an enemy in the back, betraying an ally at the right time, or revealing a weakness and exploiting it, is blessed by Shadowhand.
        Gloom is not only sorrow or melancholy, but also the deep darkness that causes it. The antithesis of luminescence, Shadowhand promotes that which smothers light, and provides obscurement. Joy and light are sickening, and They see it as naive and self-indulgent. To embrace the dark, and be sheltered by it, is Shadowhand’s wish for all mortals in Alaris


________________
Perspective
        A world without the blinding, distracting light of the sun, where all exist in a deep darkness, never having to reveal or expose their true selves, is the dream of Shadowhand. They work in Alaris to snuff out pesky sources of light and hope, bringing sorrow and despair in its place. Darkness was the original state of the world, and Shadowhand wishes for it to return.
        For in the dark and shadows, anything could be within. Since anything can exist in the dark, everything does, at least in the minds of mortals. To peel back the curtain, to sully it with light, reveals only one thing, destroying every other possibility. Being unknown and undefined, safely sheltered within the dark, in the eyes of Shadowhand, is the only way to truly be free.
Followers
Very few follow Shadowhand, and those that do almost always claim fealty to another Divine Aspect in public. Followers often work within criminal enterprises, fostering opportunism in its ranks. A few travel about as assassins and saboteurs, spreading despair as they work.
Most that follow Shadowhand do so out of selfish interest, seeking her power to elevate themselves, and then hold power over others. Interestingly, followers of Shadowhand rarely ever work together, each expecting betrayal from the other. This means that many work as lone agents.

The Father of Battle: Divine Aspect of War
Domain
        The Father of Battle is boisterous and violent, delighting in war and conflict. He is the patron of the warrior, the brute, and any who perpetuate conflict. The Father of Battle claims Fury, Conflict, and Conquering Ambition as his domain.
        No emotion burns as fierce as anger, or the fruit of anger; fury. The Father of Battle considers it to be the greatest emotion due to this. fury  is a catalyst, concentrated emotion that brings about conflicts. The anger of a revolutionary, the rage of a man slighted, or the fury of a vengeful mortal, these emotions all run hot, and lead to violence and conflict.
        The thrill of a fight, the exhilaration of a contest, these spring forth from glorious conflict. The Father of Battles sees conflict as the purest form of expression. Each battle, every war is a string of conflicts which spring from each other. A battle of wills, then strength of arms. A wonderful test for all involved, conflict separates the strong from the weak. He delights in seeing some break, and others grow strong through the struggle.
        The strong impose their will through conquering ambition. The Father of Battle respects those who have a vision for the world, and bends others towards it. The desire to have, and the willingness to take, are both indicators of strength, and are rewarded by The Father of Battle.
________________
Perspective
        Weakness, to The Father of Battle, is a sin. Only those who display strength are worthwhile, and those that do not are worth nothing. Conflict is a contest of strength, which allows both parties to test themselves, and sharpen against one another. A perfect world in His estimation would be one of perpetual war and conflict.
        A grand, unending war for control of the world is The Father of Battle’s design. He aims to stir up conflicts and strife, so that all mortals become entrenched in battle. The strength that would emerge from those that triumph would prove to be the best that mortals can achieve.
Followers
Even though He is considered an evil Divine Aspect, many follow The Father of Battle, exalting in conflicts and the betterment that results from it. Many warriors, soldiers, and generals follow Him. Conversely to The Tranquil Heart, more mortals tend to follow The Father of Battle during times of relative peace, when they begin to hunger for new territories and prosperity.
The Father of Battle’s most devoted will tend to practice a specific weapon, becoming indisputable battle-maestros. There can be only one master for each weapon, and if another wishes to become the strongest, they must defeat the reigning battle-maestro and claim their title. Each of these battle-maestros hold a legendary weapon, which was originally given to their predecessors by The Father of Battle.

The Reaper: Divine Aspect of Death
Domain
        Quiet. Looming. Inevitable. The Reaper has no real semblance of personification when compared to the other Divine Aspects. She is content to see her work done, and gives her strength to those who work alongside her to cull the living. She is queen of Decay, Undeath, and Endings.
        Where there is growth, there must be decay. All things in Alaris must eventually slide down the mortal coil, and The Reaper sees this decline as necessary. Entropy is one of the strongest things in the world, a blissful inevitability that allows that which was new to rot and fade away.
        Though The Reaper enjoys the decay and death of things, She still allows a select few to circumvent death, to pass through it and return to the inner planes as an undead creature. Though this may seem contradictory, The Reaper rules over undeath, and allows them to exist, as often these undead help to usher other mortals to their demise. Most undead require the death of other mortals to remain, meaning The Reaper always gets her due.
        All things must end. In The Reaper’s eyes, the ending is most important of all, for without an ending, nothing that comes before has meaning. Kingdoms must end, people must die, ideas must be forgotten. Endings are essential, the stilling of unquiet sounds in the universe. She watches with a still, small smile as things come to end at last.
________________
Perspective
        Life is noise, a constant droning, an unnatural state for existence. For before The Divine created Alaris, all was as it should be, as nothing truly was. The Reaper considers herself to be the truest aspect of The Divine, for she aims to return the universe to how it used to be: silent.
        She does not hate living things, but works to snuff them out nonetheless. She must, for the only way to give their lives meaning is to end them. From The Reaper’s perspective, the entire story of existence is in need of someone to end it. Because until Alaris completely ends, it cannot be finished, and will forever be incomplete, missing the final piece of the puzzle.
Followers
        Followers of The Reaper are rare, and those that do worship her are often instrumental in major catastrophes and losses of life. Storied rumors of The Reaper’s Hand are told across Alaris; a secretive group of assassins that pay tribute to The Reaper by killing mortals. They are very precise and clean, never leaving slaughter in their wake, but rather gently, almost tenderly killing their targets, and ushering their souls into the afterlife.
        The organization is led by The First Finger (Pointer, to her friends), a mortal blessed with extended life and a deadly touch. She runs The Reaper’s Hand, supposingly communing with The Reaper and sending her servants on assassination missions. Some seem completely random, while others are very specific targets. Many parents use stories of the mythical assassins to scare their children into behaving.

Geography of Alaris
        The world of Alaris is full of varied and colorful regions. The world itself is a pangea-like supercontinent, with very few individual landmasses. Within this continent are several types of environments and regions, ranging from barren mountain ranges to mired swamps, idyllic grasslands to dense forests. The purpose of this section is to give insight into these different regions and reveal any important details about them. Included with each region are a few details on the culture of that region and the most common societal structures present within them. Of course, as with any case dealing with societies and cultures, these statements are all broad impressions, and there are always exceptions. Additionally, a short description of the Planes of Alaris is included in this section. Although they are much less likely to be seen or interacted with, the planes are important to understanding the greater scope of Alaris.

Notable Regions
The Northwatch Mountains
        Located in the northernmost region of Alaris, The Northwatch Mountains are a series of large arctic mountains. The weather in this region is notoriously frigid, and the temperature rises above the freezing point only in the warmest of summers. The mountains are always covered in snow, which is blown about in storms, causing poor visibility. The name “Northwatch” comes from the vigilant watchman who would protect their towns by keeping watch through these snowstorms.
        The fauna of The Northwatch Mountains are either hardy beasts, able to dig beneath the snow for foliage, or fearsome predators, adapted to ambush their prey quickly and effectively. Both predator and prey are rather sparse, and roam about the region frequently, searching for food.
        Few folk are strong enough to make a living in these inhospitable lands. Those that do often form communities around hot springs, which allow for small pockets of life to flourish. Folk that live in the mountains are colloquially called “Northwatchers.” Most Northwatchers are tough folk, who put their stock in strength and willpower.
        Most Northwatcher families are led by the strongest individual. Every year, members of the family gather together to compete amongst themselves in games of strength. Whoever wins these games becomes the leader of the family. In this way, Northwatch families are always ruled by the strongest among them. Despite the constant competition, Northwatch families are extremely tight-knit, and it is considered extremely taboo to hold a grudge against a family member.
        Most Northwatchers are humans or dwarves, although several tribes of orcs call the mountain ranges home as well. They live difficult lives, led by a single chieftain, and often take to raiding Northwatch communities. These orcs have adapted to the frigid climate and are resistant to its chill. Several bands of orcs take to ambushing their prey from within a snowstorm.
        The most notable mountain in the region is Mount Star-Touch, which stands alone and reaches up past the clouds. According to the stories, those that can make the dangerous ascent to the peak can reach up and claim a star as their own.
The Nifterii Forests
        Surrounding the nearby Emerald Sea, The Nifterii (Elvish for “Sacred Homeland”) Forests are the ancestral homeland of the elves. The area is a temperate forest with a dense canopy of trees, and in some regions, giant stands of trees form groves that tower above the canopy.
        The Nifterii Forests are home to almost-exclusively elves. They guard their borders zealously to keep out those who would despoil their bountiful forest. The forests are primarily inhabited by wood elves who live in treetop communities, occasionally accompanied by forest gnomes. Near the coastal hills, the high elves reside in cities of beautiful architecture. There are a few settlements upon The Everquisst Mountains, which are known as the most majestic mountain ranges in Alaris.
        The Nifterii forests are home to a diverse ecosystem of both flora and fauna, with several different rare species making their homes among the plentiful foliage. The Emerald Sea can also be found in this region. This large body of freshwater is known for perfectly reflecting the light of the stars, even when on the cloudiest of nights. A special species of mollusk lives within the waters, and produces pearls composed of special gemstones, like ruby or emerald. These rare “gemstone pearls” are highly valued as a spell component or in the making of jewelry.
        Another notable landmark in the region is The Sundered Peak, a large mountain-sized crater in the earth. This was created by the ancient elven weapon Faer-Belthor during The Great War of The Ancestors, and is entirely devoid of life to this day.

The Greyslate Hills
        The Greyslate Hills are a rocky and mostly barren region with a few streams that flow through them. Though the surface has a rather limited ecosystem, the subterranean tunnels are the true bulk of the region. Deep beneath the surface, several tunnels wind through the earth, with various dwarven settlements making their homes within them.
        The Greyslate Caverns are actually the highest point in The Underdark, and several tunnels in the caverns lead down into The Underdark proper. There are an abundance of valuable metals and gemstones in the region, which the native dwarves make great use of.
        The caverns are home to a moderate variety of flora and fauna, with a few plants and creatures specially-adapted to cave living residing within. On occasion, more fearsome monsters from The Underdark venture into The Greyslate Caverns looking for easier prey and less competition.
        The Greyslate Caverns also directly connect to The Mountainhomes of the dwarves, which are the population centers of the region. Hundreds of dwarven clans reside within The Mountainhomes and the surrounding caverns.

The Coast of Hopes
        The Coast of Hopes is a long stretch of land; a medley of cliffs, grassy fields, and rocky outcrops. The region has a moderately-sized ecosystem with a fair number of plants and animals inhabiting it. The true remarkability of the region comes from its location, near the Ironbark Forest and The Sea of Spikes. Though the rocky cliffside makes coastal settlements rare, there are a few rocky beaches that are inhabited.
        Most settlements on The Coast of Hopes are fishing villages and maritime towns, which survive off The Sea of Spikes. Nearly all settlements on The Coast of Hopes are independent city-states, unaffiliated with The Kingdom of Goldhame. The largest of these city-states is Sea-Light City, which is famous for having won its independence during The Goldhame Coup.
        Sea-Light City is a maritime superpower in Alaris, using its claim on the valuable Ironwood from the nearby Ironbark Forest to construct the only ships able to safely sail the treacherous Sea of Spikes. Sea-Light City is known as a bastion of freedom in Alaris. It is well known that any who come to the city will be treated as equals, no matter their race, their past, or any other factor. This trust comes at a cost, however. Those who commit crimes in the city are branded with a mark on their foreheads, detailing the crime they committed. Despite, or perhaps because of, this callous approach to lawbreaking, the people of Sea-Light City are all unified in their purpose, and they tend to have a strong community. The city is always bustling and busy, and the Sea-Light Festival is one of the largest celebrations in Alaris.

The Unclaimed Hills
        The Unclaimed Hills are a region of rocky hills and plains, bordered on two sides by impressive mountain ranges. The region has very few patches of arable land found only near the river that flows through it, and the ecosystem is quite limited. Despite these inhospitable conditions, The Unclaimed Hills are quite populated due to valuable mineral deposits that were found beneath the earth.
        The region gets very hot and dry during the summer months, and very few species of plants and animals live along the hills. The majority of life can be found along the Southstorm river and its tributaries, which flow throughout the region.
        There are two mountain ranges that border the east and west of The Unclaimed Hills, being The Stonespine Mountains and The Heatstone Mountains. The Stonespine Mountains are dangerous, covered with jagged cliff faces and home to mountain predators. Several orc tribes are known to reside around The Stonespine Mountains. The mountains are dwarven territory, and they guard the rare few passes within the mountain range.
        The Heatstone Mountains are the smaller range, and a quirk of their arrangement has created several temperate basins, where life can truly flourish and many secluded settlements can be found. These mountains do not reach as high as The Stonespine Mountains, but are impressive nonetheless. Exclusively within these mountains, an extremely rare gemstone called “Heatstone” can be found. This highly sought-after gem emanates heat around it and is very valuable, being used as jewelry in colder regions or as a powerful spell component.

The Odorr Marshes
        The Odorr Marshes are a mostly desolate region of swamp and marshland, where several kinds of dangerous plants and animals can be found. Though the region was once teeming with life, the overzealous lizardfolk brought the native ecosystem to collapse in The Era of Prosperity. The ecosystem has only now begun to recover.
        The region has very few settlements, and only one major city. The stagnant water and filthy environment makes illness easy to come by, and the natural swamp gasses give the area a rancid stench. Very few civilized folk live in the region.
        Despite its unappealing attributes, The Odorr Marshes contain several valuable resources for those who are crafty enough to take them. Indeed, practicality and caution are the two most defining traits of those few who call the region home. A few Ghostwise halflings live in stilted villages, and lizardfolk clans claim swaths of territory as their own. There are also a few temples which have long-since been sucked into the mud where yuan-ti can be found.

Old Empire Lands
        The Old Empire Lands are a collection of two regions which were unified by leaders of The Old Empire. They are notable for their fertility, relative lack of predators, and pleasant weather, and are considered by many to be the most picturesque regions of Alaris.
The Grasslands of Dredarius were unified by The Old Empire during The Great War of The Ancestors, and was the first territory formally claimed by the faction. The area is home to idyllic grasslands, meadows, occasional forests of moderate size, and gently-rolling hills. It also contains Goldpoint Bay, a center of commerce and shipping.
The Plains of Gethzerram were annexed into The Old Empire later, during The Era of Prosperity. The lands originally belonged to the orcs, but they were driven from their homes by the members of The 3-Fold Pact. The region comprises mostly prairies and steppes, and several human settlements dot the hills. 
Not far off the coast of the region, a solitary jungle island named Narkur’s End can be found. The island is said to be extremely dangerous, and none who have explored the island have ever returned. Rumors of enormous beasts, man-eating flora, and a hidden treasure now surround the mysterious jungle island.
________________
The Weilo Islands
        The Weilo Islands are a tropical group of islands belonging to an archipelago, with a diverse and exotic ecosystem as well as a unique culture that was created independent of outside interference. First “discovered” by Narkur during The Era of Change, The Weilo Islands have provided many rare and valuable materials to the mainland. The islands themselves mostly have idyllic sunny weather, although tropical storms are common during the rainy season.
        The most notable produce of The Weilo Islands is the kozae fruit, a type of tree-grown fruit similar to a coconut, with a smooth, hard shell and soft pink flesh inside. Kozae is used in several dishes on the island, and when refined by specific alchemical processes, has hallucinogenic properties.
        Inhabitants of the islands, known as Weilo Islanders wear masks in public, considering facial expressions to be an intimate gesture, reserved for those closest to them. Their masks are all decorated to express individuality, and each mask bears a mark, denoting the spirits of the islander.
        In the culture of Weilo Islanders, The Divine Aspects are not worshiped. Instead, the islanders look to the spirits. Perhaps due to the unique effects of the kozae fruit, or a special property of the island, Weilo Islanders can see and interact with certain spirits of nature. This phenomenon is very rare on the main continent of Alaris, but spirits are supposedly quite common on The Wielo Islands.
Upon reaching adulthood, each islander chooses a type of spirit with which they identify, becoming bonded to that type for life. An islander can only see spirits with which they are bonded, and the spirits can impress general emotions onto islanders that are bonded to them.

Political Organizations of Alaris
The Kingdom of Goldhame
        The Kingdom of Goldhame is a powerful faction, composed mostly of humans, which rules over the majority of land in Alaris. The Kingdom has great amounts of power and resources at its disposal, which is derived from the four primary noble houses. These houses each control sections of territory, and hold a measure of influence on the crown.
        Each noble house has different specialties, mindsets, and resources at their disposal, although they all have something in common: ambition. Nobles of the primary houses are constantly scheming and plotting, making political plays and counter-plays, trying to win prestige, influence, and favor in the royal court.
        The Kings and Queens of Goldhame are known to grant special boons to nobility that serve them well and often offer lucrative business deals and special privileges as gifts to their favored noble houses.
        Each noble house is in charge of their territory, and administers the law within. They are tasked with defending their own territory, and the lives of those who live under their rule. The noble houses stay afloat through trade, selling resources amongst themselves and other nations, while paying a tax to the crown. A short description is listed along with these noble houses, as are the towns, cities, and villages they control. These are only the more notable settlements in Alaris, and are not a comprehensive list.

  House Gauntier
“Industry, Defying Danger”
        House Gauntier is known for its abundant industry and wealth. It is the most financially prosperous of the noble houses, and they export the majority of common trade goods in the kingdom, making profits off of their grains, lumber, and iron exports.
        House Gauntier, through its savvy business deals and deep pockets, has come to take ownership of much of the land in The Kingdom. Whenever a new settlement is bought out by House Gauniter, production increases drastically. House Gauntier is known to establish a presence in even the most dangerous environments if it means there is profit to be made.
        House Gauntier has ownership of large swaths of land and the settlements which live on it. These settlements produce large amounts of common goods and are the foundations upon which House Gauntier’s economy is built.


Ulthuan Village 
        Ulthuan Village is a smaller settlement built just outside the fringes of The Nifterii Forests. The settlement is mostly quiet, save for occasional disputes with the elves of the forest. The rare elven herbs harvested here are very valuable to apothecaries, and the villagers occasionally ship exports of lumber, taken from trees that are considered “outside the forest.”
________________


Hollowstump
        Hollowstump is a mid-sized town built entirely on stilts above a churning pit of mud. Life here is difficult, but enterprising folk can make a tidy profit, if they can stomach some danger and discomfort. The town collects valuable swamp herbs, which are known for being potent medicines (and poisons). Some brave folk of Hollowstump make a living hunting monsters in the swamplands, harvesting their valuable parts which are worth much to alchemists.
Glitterleaf Village
        Glitterleaf Village is a quiet place, with rich mines full of rare minerals. The village gets its name from these minerals, which are present in the soil. Plants that grow in this soil grow shiny, glittery leaves. The village exports their minerals to blacksmiths and alchemists, who pay good money for them.
Twin-River City
        Twin-River City is a population center first and foremost, and is the commerce hub of the region. The Rivergem Market sells exotic goods and wares from all corners of the world, while The Industry District is home to some of the best craftsmen in Alaris. The city is also the seat of House Gauntier’s power, with the magnificent Cragspray Keep overlooking the city. An intricate system of locks and dams creates The Arrowshot Canal, a fast-flowing waterway which can greatly decrease the travel time to the coast.


Splinterpost Basin
        Splinterpost Basin is one of the most profitable settlements under the control of House Gauntier, providing a variety of common resources. The Basin is a small, fertile region which produces lumber, food, and iron. The settlement was originally formed by a group of refugees which fled during The Grim Raze. These settlers eventually accepted the rule of The Kingdom of Goldhame and House Gauntier, but they set special provisions, allowing them to elect their own leaders, so long as they met their export quotas.
________________
  House Tylock
“The Manners of All Magics”
        House Tylock is known for its refined nobility and their mastery of arcane magic. Most nobles from this house are wizards, and they take their studies in the field of arcane magic very seriously. They hold a respectable amount of influence in the royal court, and they specialize in magical exports: spellbooks, magical components, and very occasionally, magic items.
The nobility of House Tylock is notoriously haughty, and they consider themselves “a cut above” other nobles. Notably, Tylock nobles dislike those who use the magic of artifice, but they tolerate it as a distasteful necessity.
        House Tylock does not have many massive swaths of land, but instead they hold a scattering of small regions, which produce important materials highly sought after by spellcasters.
Southpoint
        Southpoint is a small fishing town which is of little import to The Kingdom of Goldhame. The town produces a large amount of ocean fish and is a minor hub for maritime trade, mostly used by minor merchants and shipping companies. Notably, it is the closest port to The Weilo Islands.
Bluestrand Village
        Bluestrand Village is a small settlement in The Plains of Gethzerram, which primarily exports rare textiles with magical properties. Nearby groves are home to a rare species of silkworm that produce magical silk, which is woven into textiles. These textiles are prized by clothiers, and can be used to make magical robes.
________________


Mistfall
        Mistfall is a mid-size town known for its several archeological sites obscured by a magical mist. Many theorize this mist to be a remnant of The Grim Raze, and those who travel in it find themselves easily lost. Within this shifting mist are several ancient ruins, which date back to the peak of The Old Empire. These ruins undoubtedly hold secrets within, but they can only be rarely explored due to the mist. Relics and magical materials from these forays into the ruins are a primary export of this town.
Thornford
        Thornford is a small, unimportant farming village owned by House Tylock. It produces plenty of foodstuffs, and the citizens are (somewhat contradictorily) said to be among the most refined farmers in Alaris. 
Midside
        Midside is a moderately-sized city built on the shores of The Inner Sea. It serves primarily as a population and commerce center, and is the seat of power for House Tylock. This city has had small enchantments placed on it over the years, and has a variety of minor magical improvements to its infrastructure. Magical lights illuminate the streets at night, and the walls are enhanced with warding magic.
The Tower of Wonders
        The Tower of Wonders is a somewhat controversial settlement maintained by House Gauntier in The Inner Sea. The Tower of Wonders is a haven for inventors and artificers, who work on their ingenious and dangerous inventions within the magical walls of The Tower. This settlement demands valuable resources, but has been the birthplace of several revolutionary (and profitable) inventions. On rare occasions, artificers from The Tower of Wonders will sell magic items that they have created for a large sum.




________________
  House Dimir
“Far Reach, Firm Grasp”
        House Dimir is one of the most ambitious houses in The Kingdom of Goldhame. They are always focused on expanding their reach, and monopolizing new discoveries. When a new deposit of rare metals is found, rest assured House Dimir will have a claim to it (whether legitimate or not). House Dimir’s focus is always on a new venture, and they have influence in every corner of the kingdom.
        A defining character trait of nobility from House Dimir is their stubbornness. Once a Dimir noble has got their hands on something, be it a plot of land or a juicy secret, one will have a hard time prying it away from them.
        House Dimir has a number of carefully-chosen settlements scattered about the kingdom, many of which are disconnected from each other. House Dimir takes a rather informal approach to managing their settlements, often allowing their citizens to do as they please, as long as they keep silent about any covert operations underway by House Dimir.
Ravenpass Village
        Ravenpass Village is a settlement on the border of The Nifterii forests, which House Dimir uses to discreetly harvest goods from. These operations are impossible to keep secret, but House Dimir has implemented certain precautions to ensure the scouts cannot be traced back to them.
Frosthold
        Frosthold is a mid-sized town which is nestled in a bay near The Northwatch Mountains. Its location shields it from the worst of the bitter winds, but the settlement is still extremely cold. A few of the toughest folk come to live here, seeking isolation above comfort. The town provides rare pelts from the mountains, as well as rare fish which are only found in this region.
Copperstick Village
        Copperstick Village is a settlement built just outside the borders of Ironbark forest, which is claimed by Sea-Light City. The village is ostensibly a mining town, sending out shipments of common metals like tin and copper. In actuality, the village exists to harvest and gather small bunches of Ironwood, the valuable lumber that can create incredibly strong ships.
Tilltop Village
        Tilltop Village is a small settlement which produces foodstuffs for House Dimir. It is relatively unimportant, although it is said that there are secrets hidden beneath the strange flat-topped hills of the region.
Saltshore
        Saltshore is a moderately-sized city, which is a population and commercial center for the region. The city of Saltshore is known for its relative lawlessness and shady dealings. In its limits you can find thieves, assassins, smugglers, pirates, corrupt officials, and illicit alchemists. This is also House Dimir’s seat of power, where they maintain a carefully-presented community of individuals, called The Upper Fields, as a front for visitors.
________________
  House Meldorus
“The Foundation of Nobility”
        House Meldorus is a rather small, yet very important noble house of The Kingdom of Goldhame, in charge of administering the bureaucracy and military of The Kingdom. House Meldorus was once the line of the throne, before King Frederick broke his ties to the house upon being crowned. Though the lineage of King Frederick still holds the crown, House Meldorus is in charge of training the heirs of Goldhame and ensuring they are fit to rule.
        Because of their influence on the monarchs, House Meldorus is often treated better than the other noble houses. In turn, the other noble houses are quite persistent in their attempts to undermine House Meldorus, and they have a hard time finding allies in the royal court.
        The Settlements controlled by House Meldorus are important to the cultural heritage of The Kingdom of Goldhame, many being among the first to revolt during The Goldhame Coup. They are now important centers of history, and play key roles in the administration of The Kingdom.


Dewgrove Village
        Dewgrove Village is an idyllic farming town in The Grasslands of Dredarius, which is known as a quiet and peaceful place. It provides token amounts of lumber and foodstuffs, but its popularity with the lesser nobility means many summer homes are built here. Dewgrove is where many lesser nobles meet together to plot and scheme while the royal court is not in session.
________________


Goldhame City
        Goldhame City is the center and crown jewel of The Kingdom of Goldhame. A massive city full of life, commerce, and opportunities, Goldhame City is the home of the royal court and the throne. It is a sprawling metropolis, where all citizens of The Kingdom are welcome to visit.
Maplepass
        Maplepass is a small town, built in a quiet grove near a peaceful river. The town is home to The Royal Cartographers’ Guild, a branch of The Kingdom of Goldhame tasked with mapping all regions of Alaris. Guides from Maplepass are said to be the best in Alaris, able to get you where you need to go quickly and safely (though not cheaply).
Whiteburrow
        Whiteburrow Village is a large town, whose focus is on refining, weighing, stamping, and shipping materials to The Kingdom. Whiteburrow is in charge of maintaining the integrity of coins, and is home to the royal mint, alongside many other refineries that produce all sorts of goods.
Port Narkur
        Port Narkur is a small fishing village of little import. The town’s economy inflated during Narkur’s journeys to the Weilo Isles, with several folk flocking to the village to profit off of his new discoveries. With the disappearance of Narkur nearly 250 years ago, the village has slowly withered away, and is now little more than a husk.

The Dwarven Thanedom
The Great Thanedome of the dwarves is a powerful, if reclusive faction in Alaris. They lay claims to the expansive tunnels and rich ore beneath The Stonespine Mountains and The Greyslate Hills. Though they are very loose with the surface of their territory, The Dwarves of The Thanedom guard the entrances to their tunnels zealously.
The Dwarven Thanedom is led by a council of Thanes, one from each dwarven clan. These Thanes participate in democratic votes, which determine administration and policy for the Thanedom. Because the clans are highly independent and self-reliant, sessions of The Council of Thanes are held rarely.
Dwarves of The Thanedom have a reputation as honorable and just. All dwarves of The Thanedom follow a set of codes, called The First Laws, which are inscribed upon a massive sheet of stone in their capital city. The First Laws can never be changed, and are sacred to dwarven society.
Mirroring this tradition, dwarves of the Thanedom each keep a stone tablet in their homes, called a Ljefzana, which is an important personal keepsake. They use this tablet to keep track of their accomplishments and failures, of their friends and enemies. This Ljefzana is extremely personal, and is like a journal that chronicles a dwarves entire life. This tradition is the origin of the common expression that a dwarf’s grudge is “written in stone.”
The Dwarven Clans vary in size, some having only a few dozen members, while others have thousands. These Clans all trace their roots to the original Mountainhomes, where many maintain a presence. Many dwarven settlements are named after major events in the people’s history.


________________


Darreg-Dor
The City of Darreg-Dor is the original mountainhome of all dwarves, where they were first placed by The Divine. The city still stands to this day, a hollow mountain full of life. Industrious dwarves still mine deeper into the earth, unearthing riches of valuable minerals and metals. The First Laws are written in these caverns, and serve as an important monument to dwarvenkind. 
Hollick’s Tomb
        Hollick’s Tomb is a large dwarven town most known for its mithral forge, the birthplace of several mastercrafted mithral weapons. It was named after Hollick Greenvien, the most renown mithralsmith ever to live.
Fahland
        Fahland is a dwarven town dug into the stones, a settlement of carved hallways and rooms. This town is historic to the dwarven people as the place where dwarves were taught the art of Giant Runes. Since ancient days, this settlement has been home to the most accomplished runecrafters in the Thanedom.
Spinestone 
Spinestone is a dwarven village carved into the side of the great Stonespine mountain range. It is notable in dwarven history as the place where the Duergar broke from their kin, and descended down into the underdark. This settlement is built near a deep shaft which descends directly to the underdark. Many dwarves who live here are hunters, who profit off of the monster parts harvested in the dangerous cavern below.


________________


The Elven Monarchy
        The Elven Monarchy is an ancient and powerful government consisting of the elves of The Nifterii forests and The Everquisst Mountains. The Elven Monarchy has existed longer than all other governments, and its ancient codes can be traced back to The First Era.
        The Elven Monarchy is ruled by two monarchs of equal power: The Sun King and The Moon Queen, who lead The Heavenly Court. Each of these monarchs have authority over different aspects of their government. The Sun King rules over matters of military and the common folk of the monarchy, while The Moon Queen takes precedence in matters of arcane magic, and the elven nobility.
        Members of The Heavenly Court are appointed by The Sun King and Moon Queen, and are delegated authority and responsibility for different matters of the monarchy, being appointed to positions of power.
        The territory of The Elven Monarchy is thought to be the most serene, picturesque lands in Alaris. Many of the settlements are built around the natural beauty of the environment, and the elves of The Monarchy often incorporate the same beauty into their architecture.
        Elven society in The Monarchy centers on communion with nature, and the practice of the fine arts. Music and art are highly valued in the elven monarchy. The long lifespans of the elves allow them plenty of time to practice and perfect these arts, and many are the undisputed masters of the craft.
________________


Ered-Thor
        Ered-Thor is the capital of The Elven Monarchy, and the home of The Heavenly Court. The large city is built on the slopes of The Everquisst Mountains, scattered with majestic marble towers across the skyline. The sounds of music can be heard faintly throughout the city, and the walls of homes are adorned with art.
Starglade
        Starglade is an elven town on the northern shores of the continent. The town is known to be serene, and the annual Starlantern festival draws elven celebrants from across The Monarchy.
Numon
        The town of Numon is established at the edge of The Emerald Sea, and is a common stop for wood elves and high elves alike. The town was once built around an enormous sacred tree, called The Auldtree, before The Great Diaspora. When the elves finally made it back to The Material Plane, they found the town under occupation by the dwarves, and the Auldtree cut down. They reclaimed Numon, but the stump of The Auldtree still remains, a bitter reminder of the dwarves' actions long ago.
Silverbranch
        Silverbranch is a coastal village which is home to some of the most renowned silversmiths in The Monarchy. The town is characterized by its hatred of undead, and serves as a headquarters for many of the most famous elven ghost hunters in Alaris.

The Planes of Alaris
        The world of Alaris, as known to most, is in fact only one plane of existence present in reality. Several other planes exist around, near, or even within Alaris. The purpose of this section is to describe these planes, what creatures may hail from them, and how they interact with the material plane of Alaris.


The Inner Planes
        The Inner Planes of Alaris consist of the easiest Planes to access by mortals. Creatures that are able to travel the Planes can reasonably reach any of the Inner Planes. 


The Material Plane
        The Material Plane of Alaris is thought by many to be the only plane of existence. It is here that most every creature lives their lives in material bodies. When The Divine was shattered, two new planes were created. The impact of the event caused two replicas of The Material Plane to form, one being The Feywild, and the other, The Shadowfell.


________________
The Feywild
        The Feywild is a whimsical, bright plane. It mirrors The Material Plane, with a few notable changes. The Feywild is, as the name suggests, a wild plane. Forests grow vibrantly, sprouting plants of all colors. The waters of The Feywild seem almost prismatic in their reflections. The Feywild is never dark, even by night the distant stars provide ample light. In the Feywild, The Mage Moon (Called The Fey Moon) glows a brilliant purple, and creatures that spend their time in The Feywild may find themselves absorbing some of The Fey Moon’s strange power.
        The Feywild is a place of revelry, mischief, and goodness. The native creatures are known as Seelie Fey. Most Seelie Fey are good-aligned. Creatures like Fairies, Satyrs, and Pixies are Seelie Fey.


The Shadowfell
        The Shadowfell is the second “splinter” of The Material Plane, being the inverse of The Feywild. The Shadowfell is a dark and dreary place, filled with shadows and drained of light and life. In The Shadowfell, forests are twisted, thorny growths of ash-gray trees. The waters of this plane are dull voids, reflecting no light. The Mage Moon (Called The Dark Moon) seems more of an outline, an eclipse of the moon, emanating a dread aura. Creatures that spend their time in this plane are infused with this dark power, which leeches the color from their skin, hair, and eyes, and wrenches their very emotions away from them.
        Creatures native to The Shadowfell are known as Unseelie Fey. Darklings, Hags, and Shadar-Kai are all Unseelie Fey, and are typically evil-aligned.


________________
The Ethereal Plane
        The Ethereal Plane surrounds and permeates The Material Plane, and is thought to be the native plane of Souls and Aether. When a living creature dies, their soul passes to The Ethereal Plane, and then to the afterlife. Creatures can also use Aether to access The Ethereal Plane. Because all planes in Alaris are connected closely to The Ethereal Plane, it can be used by powerful individuals as a way to traverse The Planes.
The Ethereal Plane has two parts: The Border Ethereal, and The Deep Ethereal. A creature in The Border Ethereal is unable to interact with the planes, and creatures of The Planes (typically) are unable to see or interact with creatures inhabiting The Border Ethereal, and vice versa. The Deep Ethereal is connected to all Inner Planes, and living creatures that reach The Deep Ethereal can travel to the Inner Planes. Only the Souls of The Dead are permitted to pass to the Outer Planes using The Deep Ethereal.


The Elemental Planes
        The Elemental Planes are the four planes which represent the four base Elements: Fire, Air, Water, and Earth. Each Elemental Plane is home to various shapes and sizes of Elementals, as well as Genies, which rule The Elemental Planes.
When The Divine was shattered, The Divine Aspect of Elements formed these planes, and The Divine Aspect of Life gave each plane a small seed of life. The planes grew around these seeds, each developing living creatures, shaped by the powerful forces of the elements. 
________________
The Plane of Fire
        The Plane of Fire is among the most unforgiving of The Elemental Planes, consisting of sweltering deserts, rivers of lava, a never-setting sun, and volcanic peaks. Embers rain across the plane constantly, sweeping across the landscape in mighty storms of fire and smoke. Water cannot be found here naturally, making it near-impossible for mortal creatures to survive. Creatures native to this plane include Fire Elementals, Magma and Smoke Mephits, Azers, Salamanders, and are ruled by Efreet, who construct utilitarian palaces of bronze.


The Plane of Air
        The plane of Air is a very unique Elemental plane, being that there is almost no solid ground. This plane consists near-entirely of swirling air currents, vast clouds, and raging thunderstorms. The only solid surfaces in The plane of Air are clouds that have been bound together, usually by Djinni. Creatures native to this plane are Air Elementals, Smoke and Dust Mephits, Aracokra, and Djinni, who live in whimsical palaces built atop storm clouds.


The Plane of Water
        The plane of Water consists almost entirely of an endless ocean. The surface of this ocean is a mass of icebergs, shifting and colliding with each other on the roiling waves. Beneath the waves, a vibrant and diverse ocean is filled with life. Immense coral reefs sprawl across the shadows, while deeper, darker waters are home to more malicious creatures. No one knows how deep the waters of this plane truly go. Many creatures call The Plane of Water home, including Water Elementals, Merrow, Tritons, Ice and Mud Mephits, and Marid, who construct extravagant palaces of colorful coral.
The Plane of Earth
        The Plane of Earth is an endless expanse of tunnels, caverns, and soil. There is no surface in this plane, only stone and earth. The labyrinthine tunnels are constantly shifting due to the constant earthquakes of the plane, and creatures that hail from another plane are in severe danger without a guide. Hardy creatures call this plane home, such as Earth Elementals, Xorn, Umber Hulks, Magma, Dust and Mud Mephits, as well as Dao, who rule from their ostentatious palaces of gemstones and precious metals.


The Outer Planes
        The further one ventures from The Material Plane, which was created to be the cradle of mortal life, the more abstract and bizarre the planes become to mortal comprehension. They were created after The Divine was shattered, each one intended to be a final destination for followers of The Divine Aspects. Ever since The Divine Aspects were imprisoned in The Godspire, their power sustaining The Outer Planes has grown weaker. Souls in The Outer Planes eventually fade away over the course of about a few centuries. If a Soul is destroyed in The Outer Planes, it is well and truly destroyed. The Outer Planes are the farthest known planes in Alaris. Beyond them is unknown.


Celestus
        Celestus is The Plane of Good, and is the final resting place for the souls of good-aligned creatures. This plane consists of idyllic countrysides, serene forests, breathtaking mountains, and cities of pure marble. Souls that come here are given a body most fitting with their wishes, that can suffer no illness, and does not age. Some souls may choose to take up the mantle of Celestial, extending the time it will take for their souls to fade. Celestus was created by The Divine Aspects of Life, Light, Law, and Peace.
Avernus
        Avernus is The Plane of Evil, the destination of evil-aligned creatures. The plane is a wasteland covered in smoke and decay, rivers of flesh-warping ichor running throughout. Souls that arrive here become Fiends, beings whose form shifts and power increases as they obtain mortal souls. Fiends can kill each other to grow stronger, and can also use their power to contact mortals in The Inner Planes, making deals for their souls in exchange for wealth or power.  Mortals that sell their soul to a Fiend do not travel to The Outer Planes upon their death. Typically, fiends that kill for their power are called Demons, and those that make deals with mortals are called Devils, and the distinction is usually made clear in the forms they take. Demons adapt their forms as they gain more power, becoming fierce creatures of destruction, while Devils change their forms to better communicate and beguile Mortals, as well as control any Fiends which come under them. Avernus was created by The Divine Aspects of Darkness, War, and Death.
________________
Mechanus
        Mechanus is The Plane of Law, the destination of lawful-aligned creatures. This plane is a perfectly symmetrical series of rings, each orbiting around a central sphere. Souls that arrive here are called Modrons (No relation to the weird geometric creatures), and are given a mechanical body with a single identifying mark of their choice, be it a hairstyle, eye color, or tattoo. Besides the identifying mark, all Modrons in Mechanus are identical. Modrons are set to work in specific roles, tending gardens on the inner rings, constructing housing on the outer rings, or replacing the components which keep the rings orbiting. Life in Mechanus is stable and predictable. Each Modron works a set amount of years in their position before being promoted. Every Modron works as a precise cog in a grand, planar machine. Each and every Modron, before they fade from The Outer Planes, will be promoted to the role of Primus, the leader of Mechanus. By working in the strict scheduled bounds placed upon them, every soul that becomes a Modron will have a chance to wield near-deific power, if only for a short time, before they fade from The Outer Planes. Mechanus was created by the Divine Aspects of Trade and Magic.
________________
lzkedubus
        Izkedubus is The Plane of Chaos, the unpredictable destination of chaotic-aligned creatures. To try to describe this plane is futile, every moment the terrain shifts, spreads, fractures, and collides. The ground beneath your very feet can change from desert to ocean, then to nothing at all, all while the air you breathe becomes smoke, while gravity inverses. Izkedubus is complete and utter unpredictable chaos. Souls that wind up here are given a body that they design themselves, and can change at will. These souls can exert their will upon the plane around them, changing matter however they can imagine. The more creative they are, the more powerful a soul here is. Souls in Izkedubus lose their memories the longer they remain in the plane, being forced to constantly reinvent themselves and their environment. By the time they fade from The Outer Planes, most souls have no memory of their life on The Material Plane. Izkedubus was created by The Divine Aspects of Elements and Magic.

Life In Alaris
        As I mentioned before, Alaris is a vanilla fantasy world. Even though the foundations are familiar, I believe there are some interesting and unique facets of this world that set it apart from other vanilla fantasy worlds. This section is intended to shed some light upon some of these novelties in the world which separate it from others of its like. The foremost of these is time. In Alaris, time is always moving. Seasons come and go, holidays are celebrated, the moon shifts in the sky, and factions go about achieving their goals. The most important thing about Alaris is the passage of time, how history is being written constantly.
        The Alaris Standard Calendar is currently in the year 10,158 (called 158 Post Reform). The year consists of 300 days, divided into 8 months. Each month has 5 weeks, each of which is 5 days long, for a total of 25 days to a month. The spring months are Sprout and Soil, the summer months are Glimmer and Blaze. Fall and Winter are Wither and Fade, then Frost and Thaw, respectively. You can find the calendar (with holidays and moon cycles) here.
Also listed in this section are a few pages of information on the common currencies of Alaris and languages spoken in the world.

Common Holidays and Celebrations
        The people of Alaris have enough of a shared history that most groups of civilization celebrate common holidays. While each community, race, and culture might have their own holidays, the most commonly-celebrated holidays are listed here.
Winter’s Leave - Thaw 12
        A minor festival, Winter's Leave is a send-off to the winter months and their chill. Celebrations are small community gatherings with warm stew and mead served around a fire.
Festival of First Bloom - Sprout 5
        A minor festival celebrating the arrival of spring. The main celebration involves a flower competition, with participants competing to see who has the best bouquet. The winner traditionally donates the bouquet to a Divine Aspect of their choice, which is said to decide what type of blessing the town will receive for the year.
Festival of New Youth - Sprout 23-25
        A major festival celebrating life, youth, and happiness. This 3-day festival is a time of carefree joy and revelry, and folk of all ages are encouraged to embrace their youthful tendencies. The festival is a time of abundant pranks and mischief, and revelers wear colorfully painted masks to obscure their identity. The only way to be excluded from the constant pranks is to wear a black mask, in which case you must provide celebrators either candy or coins if they happen to request it.
________________
Neighbor’s Field Day - Soil 8
A minor celebration of community and common goals, this tradition originated in farming villages, where neighbors would spend their days helping each other plant their fields, then hosting a potluck at the end of the day. This celebration remains near its original purpose in farming villages, but the day of community and goodwill has become popular in other settlements as well. These settlements tend to celebrate the day with acts of kindness and charity, as well as each resident giving a small gift to their nearest neighbors.
Highsun Games - Glimmer 17
A major festival which serves as a contest of skills, The Highsun Games are a series of trials and contests to determine The Highsun Champion for that year. The events are typically organized by a community leader, and the games are usually good-natured contests of physical and mental skills. Common games include a race, duel, archery contest, and riddle contest, although especially creative organizers can add whatever they deem fit. Crowds watch on and cheer competitors, while wagers are placed on the outcome of the games.
Day of Reform - Wither 4
        A major festival established and regulated by The Kingdom of Goldhame, The Day of Reform celebrates the end of the bloody civil war, which was ended by a violent coup led by Frederick Goldhame, the founder of The Kingdom of Goldhame. This festival is typically celebrated with a kingdom-sponsored fair in the center of town, always revolving around a group of traveling performers who illustrate The Goldhame Coup in a play. Merchants take advantage of the festival as well, setting up stalls selling concessions and trinkets.
Day of The Divine - Fade 7
A small celebration of religious importance, the Day of The Divine celebrates the various Divine Aspects. Most who celebrate this day spend it in worship, performing the tenets of an aspect (or multiple) that they follow, and donating to the relevant temples.
Harvest Festival - Fade 18
        A festival celebrating the year's labor, some form of a Harvest Festival can be found in nearly every settlement. Celebration usually includes a large social event and feast. Oftentimes in small-population settlements this event doubles as an opportunity for residents to reaffirm their trust in their local leader, or elect a new one.
Spirit’s Rising - Frost 18
        A minor festival to temporarily ward off the bleak feeling of winter, Spirit's Rising usually begins as a somber morning, with anticipation slowly ramping up, climaxing in a celebration around a large bonfire. Usually celebrations carry on well into the night, and alcoholic drinks are considered essential to the celebration.

Alaris Unique Materials
        The world of Alaris has several materials that you might find in the real world. From metals like Iron and Copper, to wood like Oak or Birch, you can assume most real-world materials exist in Alaris. There are, however, some materials that are completely fantastical, and usually confer some special benefits. Here are some of them as well as their unique properties.
Ironwood
        Ironwood is a rare type of wood only found interspersed among the trees of the aptly-named Ironwood Forest. It is most notably used in naval construction due to its unique properties. When freshly-cut, Ironwood is soft and pliable. Once the wood completely dries, however, it locks its shape and becomes incredibly strong. 
The wood makes excellent ships, but also can be used in wooden weapons. When you attack with an Ironwood weapon, you double your strength modifier when dealing damage. (As an example, Draven, who has a Strength Modifier of +3 is fighting with an Ironwood Club. When he wrecks a poor goblin, he rolls 1d4+6 damage, instead of 1d4+3.)
Ironwood is also commonly used to create sturdy shields. An Ironwood shield has the normal +2 AC bonus, but prevents its wielder from taking double damage from critical hits, treating any crit on its wielder as a normal hit.
________________
Dragonsteel
        Dragonsteel is an incredibly rare metal, which can only be created by a dragon’s breath weapon. It is only used in weapons, and can hold an edge incredibly well. Dragonsteel always holds strata-like essence in the metal which indicate the type of dragon that forged the material. 
Dragonsteel weapons function as normal weapons, but a perfect strike can cause the magic within them to flare out in force. When you roll a Natural 20 on an attack roll using a Dragonsteel weapon, you add additional damage of the corresponding type (fire, acid, poison, lightning, or cold) equal to 1d8 times your proficiency modifier.
Aetherium
        Aetherium is a rare kind of crystal found only in the Northwatch Mountains. It is extremely sensitive to magic and can absorb great amounts of Aether. The crystal gives off a magical blue light, which counts as dim light for 5 feet. As the crystal absorbs Aether, it grows at an extremely slow pace, expanding its crystalline form using pure magical energy. The largest known crystal of Aetherium was reported to be about the size of a fist.
        The absorbed Aether within this special crystal can be extracted by spellcasters, which destroys the crystal in the process. A creature with the ability to cast spells can use a bonus action to destroy an Aetherium crystal that they are holding, restoring their natural magical energy stores. A creature that draws power from a crystal regains spell slots depending on the size of the crystal.
* Tiny: 1 level worth of spell slots
* Small: 3 levels worth of spell slots
* Medium: 5 levels worth of spell slots
* Large: 7 levels worth of spell slots
* Huge: 10 levels worth of spell slots
________________
Faetree Sap
        Faetree Sap is a slightly magical substance which is extracted from the Faetree, a rare tree which grows in The Nifterii Forests. Faetrees are strange, willowy trees with iridescent leaves, and often seem to attract Fey creatures that wander the material plane. The sap of the trees is a clear substance which exhibits strange properties when exposed to fire. 
In its raw form, a small drop of Faetree sap, when lit on fire, produces a small, slow-moving flame that provides light equivalent to a candle but no heat. Larger amounts of sap can give light equal to a lantern or campfire.
When the Sap is refined and processed into a liquid, it instead gives off a wild and fast-flickering flame, which gives off no light but immense heat, and burns for up to an hour in the right conditions.
        Weapons, armor, and equipment can be coated in either raw or refined Faetree sap. Raw Faetree Sap allows the item to be ignited with a slow-moving, heatless flame, giving off 20 feet of bright light and 20 feet of dim light. Weapons can be dipped in Refined Faetree Sap, giving them an additional 3d4 fire damage on hit.
Mithral
        Mithral is a rare pale-green metal, which can be found in the stonespine mountains. The metal is primarily mined and refined by dwarves, who are considered masters of the rare metal. Mithral is a light metal, which is used in the making of powerful armors. Mithral absorbs most vibration and is completely silent when impacting with other pieces of mithral. This makes it especially useful in the creation of armor, as it makes lightweight and quiet protection.
        Armor made of Mithral does not give disadvantage on stealth checks, has no strength requirement to use, and weighs half the weight of a normal set of equivalent armor. (As an example, plate armor requires a strength of 15, gives disadvantage on stealth checks, and weighs 65 pounds. Mithral plate armor has no requirements, does not affect stealth checks, and weighs only 32 pounds.)

Factions of Alaris
        The world of Alaris has several factions inhabiting it, bringing politics, prosperity and strife along with them. The factions of Alaris all have their own goals and resources. Characters may find their goals to align with these factions, and joining or cooperating with these factions may help the characters and give them access to resources. This section lists the major factions, as well as their goals and resources. The listed factions here are the ones most likely to be interacted with by players.
        It is my intention for factions to be used in such a way as to add to the setting and story of the world. While certain characters go about the world, their goals may at times align with, or get in the way of these factions. They exist to help the world feel active and alive. While time passes, these factions may be looking to progress their own goals, often in ways that can affect the story.
________________
  The Kingdom of Goldhame
        The Kingdom of Goldhame is currently the largest ruling body in Alaris, and lays claim to much of the mainland. The Kingdom was founded in year 1 PR, and has been steadily growing to reclaim the land once occupied by The Old Empire. The Kingdom of Goldhame is ruled by a hereditary monarchy, and is built on the foundation of four noble houses: Gauntier, Meldorus, Tylock, and Dimir. These noble houses curry for the king's favor, and serve his will.
Goals: Stabilize and Prosper
        The Kingdom of Goldhame, though a mighty and powerful organization, is still struggling to fill the shadow of The Old Empire. The Kingdom’s economy is damaged, and the races of elves and dwarves refuse any official contact with The Kingdom, due to the treachery of The Old Empire. The Kingdom’s current focus is on securing it’s grasp on frontier settlements, to pave the way for further expansion into the area previously settled by The Old Empire
Resources: The Weight of a Kingdom
        The Kingdom of Goldhame has extensive resources at its disposal, including trained military forces, major economic power, and several established keeps, castles, and military outposts. Most citizens of The Kingdom do not enjoy these benefits, but all citizens are promised protection from outside threats as well as justice, in exchange for loyalty and taxes. Important members of The Kingdom, such as agents, politicians, or other valuable members may enjoy appropriate benefits, such as exemption from laws or punishments, temporary command of military forces or structures, or financial boons.


________________
The Freeswords Company
        A coalition of mercenaries, The Freeswords Company is a widespread organization. At nearly every town and city in Alaris, you can find a Freeswords Guildhouse, where members of the organization offer their services. Most often members of The Freeswords are offered jobs exterminating monsters, guarding facilities, or supplementing local military forces. The organization was founded during The Era of Change, and is currently sponsored by House Gauniter.
Goals: Money and Merit
        The Freeswords Company contains a diverse selection of people, all with different motives. The primary goal of The Company is to make profit for those who choose the life of a mercenary, and to cultivate a reliable reputation. Failure is serious for Freeswords, and breaching or failing a single contract is grounds for expulsion.
Resources: The Best of The Best
        The Freeswords Company has several resources at their disposal. They are in control of several highly trained mercenary groups, skilled instructors, and guildhouses across Alaris. Members are offered cheap room and board, a steady source of contracts, and access to trainers and equipment in exchange for a cut of all profits. Members also make great use of the faction's sterling reputation.
________________
The Shadow Network
        An organization of thieves, assassins, and smugglers, The Shadow Network is an organized and coordinated faction with influence in every corner of Alaris. The Shadow Network employs several spies to keep them informed of events across the world, and often takes advantage of disorder and crisis, using chaos as an opportunity to further its power.
Goals: Fingers In Everything
        The Shadow Network holds power, wealth, and information as its most valuable assets, and is always greedily looking for more. The Network holds no punches, and uses any and every underhanded trick to get what they want. To The Shadow Network, nothing is sacred. They are especially interested in monopolizing illegal operations, and they bring down anyone who does not bow to them and their rules.
Resources: Extralegal Expertise
        The Shadow Network has little to no resources in military power or armed forces, but its wealth of information and connections are made available to its members, for a price. Every transaction within The Network is based on fair trade, members trading secrets and goods in exchange for services or other resources. Members of The Shadow Network have access to safehouses scattered across Alaris, and are given access to smuggling networks, trained thieves, and assassins. Members of The Shadow Network must uphold certain rules and laws, and cannot move against each other. If a member wrongs another member, they are expelled from The Shadow Network, and are hunted by other members.


________________
The Heraldic Order
        A group of devout priests and religious proselytizers, The Heraldic order is a nomadic organization committed to spreading the influence and authority of the Divine aspects of Light, Life, Peace,  and Law. This organization uses its members and resources to provide charity and establish places of worship for any settlement they see as needing it. Once, The Heraldic Order existed simply as intermediaries between the mortal races and The Divine Aspects, but since the banishing of The Godspire, their duty has shifted.
Goals: Goodness Above Self
        The Heraldic Order strives to bring about the tenets of the Divine Aspects of Light, Life, Peace, and Law, and to help beat back the darkness in Alaris. They also seek to bring others to worship their deities, and they accept volunteers of any race or background. Seeing as The Divine Aspects cannot directly interfere in Alaris, The Heraldic Order has made it their duty to work in the best interest of their Divine Aspects
Resources: Divine Devotees
        The Order has an array of devout healers, holy warriors, and other dedicated members, ready to be deployed wherever they may be needed. They accept donations, but never charge for their aid, which means they usually lack the funds needed to supply their members with financial support. Members of The Order can make use of the organization’s clerics and religious experts, and can expect a good rapport with the common folk.


________________
The Circle of Scholars
        The Circle of Scholars is an academic organization which is ruled by a committee of scholars and magic-users. The Circle has influence among scholars and mages of all kinds. They are an organization loyal to The Kingdom of Goldhame, and have the authority to regulate the use of magic therein. The Circle of Scholars have advanced the studies of arcane magic far, and have discovered and defined the natural laws of aether. Their work directly led to the golden age of wizardry during The Era of Prosperity. The Circle of Scholars originated in The Old Empire, although they survived The Goldhame Coup by pledging their loyalty to The Kingdom instead.
Goals: Knowledge is Power
        The Circle of Scholars hold the pursuit of knowledge as both the most important, and most dangerous thing one could do. They accept that risks must be made to progress their understanding of the arcane, but also try to minimize that risk as much as possible. Recent movements in the faction have led them to research further into the fields of Rune Magic and its relationship to Aether.
Resources: Masters of The Arcane
        Nowhere in Alaris will you find better practitioners of arcane magic. The members of The Circle of Scholars devote their lives towards the practice and perfection of the arcane arts. Members of this faction can expect expert aid and advice with all matters regarding arcane magic. The Circle of Scholars has vast numbers of instructors, historians, and researchers in its ranks, all of which can be called upon by fellow members (assuming you are in good favor, or if they view your request as more important than their own work).

Currency of Alaris  
Copper: 
        Copper coins are the smallest denomination of coins in Alaris, and are used for minor transactions. Common food and drinks, unprocessed materials, street vendor goods, and simple clothing can be easily traded for in exchange for this coin. Though it varies by location, most unskilled laborers earn a few copper pieces for a day’s work.
        These coins are lightweight and made from copper. The age of a coin can be determined by how oxidized the copper has become. The coins are minted in small rectangular bars, with a punched hole on one end. Opposite the hole is a stamped crown on both sides of the bar. Each side of the stick has 5 notches so the coin can be broken into smaller pieces if needed. This is widely regarded as an outdated function, and not many use it today. Copper coins are commonly referred to as “Sticks,” “5-bits,” or “Beggars’ coin.”


Silver:
        Silver coins are a common coin to find in Alaris and are used in more important transactions. A stay at a fancy inn, a pair of boots, forged-metal tools, and other goods and services can be traded  in exchange for a silver coin. Most skilled laborers, craftsmen, or common traders can expect to earn a few silver pieces for a day’s work.  
        Silver coins are concave domes made of silver. On the interior of the dome, written in Iktuni, the scholar’s language, is the poem “Glimmer of Truth'.” On the outside of the dome is a stamped pupil. The coins are considered to be closely related to The Illuminator, the Divine Aspect of Life. These coins can be referred to as “Glimmers,” or “Light’s coin.”
________________
  

Electrum:
        Electrum is a unique coin, and is quite rare to find outside of areas which are not under the control of The Kingdom of Goldhame. The coin is used almost exclusively by sailors and those who live near The Coast of Hope or The Wielo Islands. Electrum is worth 2 silver pieces, and can be used to buy similar things as silver.
        Electrum coins are H-shaped coins with a single score down the center, allowing the coin to be broken in half. The coins are made of an alloy of gold and silver,  giving them a pale-gold color. The corners of the coin are imprinted with floral patterns. The far edge of these coins each have a different engraving, written in Ozari, the sailor’s language. One side reads “Freedom” and the other “Passion.” These coins are often called “Freecoins” or “Sailor’s coin.” The nature of Electrum coins are specifically to provide financial stability for nations outside of The Kingdom of Goldhame. Because of this, Electrum coins are not allowed to be traded in The Kingdom.


Gold:


        Gold is a very valuable coin and only common to the well-off citizens of Alaris. Gold coins can be used for most transactions of rare goods, specialty materials, and many luxury items. A talented merchant or specialist can expect to earn a few of these coins in a day’s work.  
Gold Coins are simple disks, one side engraved with The Crown of Goldhame, the other, the face of King Frederick Goldhame, the first king. Gold coins are made entirely of gold, although some have tried to sell counterfeits with only a thin gold coating. Gold coins can be referred to as “Imperials” or “Noble’s Coin.”
  

Platinum:
        The most valuable coin, Platinum pieces are rarely used, and even the most well-off nobles of Alaris don’t often use them for transactions. The few who spend platinum coins do so usually to buy land or other property. 
Platinum coins were only minted by The Old Empire, and each coin is unique in shape, size, and design. They all weigh roughly the same, and are usually worth around ten gold pieces each, although some are worth more, and others less, depending on the design. Many affluent nobles collect these coins as a display of wealth.

Languages of Alaris
Common
        Common is the most widely-spoken language in the world (as the name would suggest). At least a few words of it are spoken by almost every person who lives in a non-isolated society. The language is an amalgam of other languages, but is very simple to learn. It has a spoken and written form, and both are commonly used. Common is most often used as a trade language, and has several precise naming conventions for weights, measures, and values.
Elvish
        Elvish is a language of flowing, soft words, invented by the elves, and has existed for thousands of years with little change. The language is rare to hear outside of elven lands, though once it was much more widespread. It has both a written and spoken form. Elvish has many words for things of beauty, and several poetic adjectives, making it especially versatile to employ in music and song.
Dwarvish
        Dwarvish is a language full of practical and bold letters and enunciations, written and spoken primarily by dwarves. The language is not seen as often as Common, but is employed more than Elvish. The language is known to contain several useful words to be applied in military context, and many generals learn the language.
________________
Halfling
        Halfling is a subtle language with no written form. It is used almost exclusively by halflings, and is a wonderful storytelling language thanks to its inclusion of several types of descriptive words for colors and sensations (especially taste). Few hear this language outside of a halfling’s living room.
Giant
        Giant is a low, thrumming language written in the runic script, and spoken only by giantkind. The language is uncommon to see anywhere, although it has a striking similarity to the magical Great Runes taught to the dwarves ages ago.
Orcish
        Orcish, like its speakers, has had to adapt and change much over the passage of time. The language has both a written and spoken form. It has many descriptive words for plants, animals, and survival, making it a valuable language to rangers, hunters, and trackers.
Goblinoid
        Golbinoid is a language devised by The Divine Aspects of Death, Darkness, and War as a way for their creations to communicate with one another. It has a spoken and a written form, neither of which are often used outside of goblin or hobgoblin camps. The language has an enormous selection of descriptive terms for murder, assault, and other forms of violence, as well as many inventive curses.
________________
Gnomish
        The Gnomish language was invented by gnomes, who wanted to have a form of communication better suited to scientific terms to discuss their inventions among each other. The language has a written and spoken form, and is full of terminology useful to the sciences. Eventually, speakers of this language would go on to invent Iktuni, the scholar’s script, although several gnomes still speak gnomish.
Sylvan
        Sylvan is the language of the fey, both seelie and unseelie. It originated from a combination of Elvish and a proto-language of the Fey, which were combined together in the years following The Great Diaspora. The language has only a spoken form, and is well suited to the description of emotions and feelings.
Primordial
        Primordial is the language of elementals, and has only a spoken form. It is a bare-bones language, only able to communicate simple ideas with little nuance. It has four subscripts, each of which can be used in combination with Primordial to better communicate in the language. Ignan has several verbs describing actions and deeds, while Terran contains many nouns to be used in speaking of objects. Aquan makes use of prepositions and conjunctions, linking together disparate objects and actions, while Auran has several descriptive adjectives to better convey and explain thoughts and ideas. When used together, the sublanguages of Primordial function as a single chaotic, sometimes nonsensical, yet strangely beautiful language.
________________
Infernal
        Infernal is the language of demons and devils, which is rarely spoken outside of Avernus. It is a harsh, grating language that is difficult to pronounce. It has both a written and spoken form. In Infernal, words can have several different meanings, many of them being duplicitous and difficult to truly decipher. The nature of this language helps devils entrap mortal souls into their contracts.
Draconic
        Draconic is an ancient language, first spoken by the true dragons. It has a written and spoken form, although only true dragons can pronounce many of the words. The language has many words for dominion and control of territory.
Celestial
        Celestial is the language spoken by the creatures of Celestus, and is very rarely spoken in the other planes. The language has several words to describe good deeds and joy. The language has only a spoken form, which lends itself very poorly to translating into other languages, leading to words of prophecy and divine visions being especially confusing and vague.
Ozari
        Ozari is the sailor’s script, a written language devised by the people of Sea-Light City, and adopted by many coastal settlements and cultures. The language has many descriptive terms for seafaring terminology, and has a simple symbology which can be used to denote the purposes of locations. Many seaside taverns and warehouses are marked with symbols from this language, so sailors can read them no matter their spoken language.
Iktuni
        Iktuni is known as the scholar’s script, and exists only in written form. It is an efficient language which can convey many ideas in a short amount of letters. The composition of the script allows a great degree of precision when speaking of scientific matters and topics. This language was derived from gnomish, and is now in use all across the world.
Old Imperial
        Old Imperial was once the language of humans, before Common became widely adopted as standard. It was spoken for centuries in The Old Empire, and underwent several variations during its use. The language is well-rounded, although it has few terms to describe magic, as the leaders of The Old Empire did not wish for it to be discussed among the common folk. Old Imperial has a spoken and written form.
Wielo
        The Wielo language has drastically different grammatical structure compared to the other languages of Alaris, due to its speakers living in isolation from the rest of the world for several thousands of years. The language has many rhythmic breaks in its pronunciation, which often blurs the line between individual words in the eyes of those trying to learn the language.
Northtounge
        The language of Northtounge is a spoken language common in the Northwatch Mountain region. It is a simple language which conveys ideas in an efficient manner, often in very few words. The language originated from the isolated communities of Northwatchers.
Speekin’
        Speekin’ is a sublanguage of Common, bastardized by mispronunciations and contractions. It originated among the communities of street urchins in the larger cities, and is a surprisingly clever way to communicate information that would be important to urchins: Locations of food, the moods of people, easy marks, and good shelters. Thieve’s Cant was inspired by this language, although it was designed with an emphasis on subtlety.
Druidic
        Druidic is the language of nature, and has a spoken and written form. It is primarily learned by druids, and one cannot understand the language unless they understand nature as well. The language cannot be taught, but can be learned by those with an open mind. The language is hard to notice when it is written on trees and plants unless one knows what to look for, as the composition of the script is so natural.
