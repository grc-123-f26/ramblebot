Wolves of Fenris did not flee their prey. No order to retreat was given.

It was felt – like an instinct of the pack, knowing when the hunt must end.

Bjorn’s chassis groaned as he turned on his waist axis, frame scarred and pitted, spattered with blood and gore.

‘The battle is done,’ Bjorn intoned, his voice heavy with centuries.

One by one, the Space Wolves yielded ground, bolters barking as they sent the last of the outcasts to their graves. The whine of chainswords guttered out. Atmund led his Grey Hunters across the corpse-strewn ground to where Bjorn stood, alone in a ring of ruin. Through the drifting smoke they saw a charnel house – ice-dark rockcrete awash with blood and shattered flesh.

As the Space Wolves pulled back from the ruins of Purgatum, silence fell among them.

It was the heavy weight of survival. The silence of knowing vengeance was still owed.

Their thoughts strayed inwards, towards what had been lost. Duty and grief braided into the same bitter song, a dirge as old as Fenris itself.

The Death Guard had stolen one warrior’s gene-seed, and in return they had burned an entire apothecarion’s worth to ashes. But Khalpax Vigorex yet lived. And now they had the death of three Wolf Guard to avenge.

The wolves of retribution did not sleep. Bjorn knew this well. More work waited when fate next gave them the chance.

Sensing their mood, he raised his voice.

‘We held the field of slaughter,’ Bjorn declared. ‘We’ve taught those rune-breakers the price of messing with the sons of Russ!’

The Space Wolves’ heavy steps quickened at his voice.

‘You must have fought much greater battles than these,’ Hrutur said quickly.

‘Yes,’ Bjorn boomed at last, ‘a few lost and many won. All of them bloody. But this was a good battle. You fought well, each of you. Russ would be proud to name you his sons!’

In the gloom, they saw the Thunderhawk cut towards them, running lights cold and blue against the dark. It came in fast and low, counter-thrusters howling, plumes of snow and ice blasted into spirals beneath it. The engines thundered, then wound down to a growl as the craft settled on its undercarriage, each clawed foot biting deep into the frozen flats.

Its turrets swept the wastes, searching for threats. But there were none. There were many bodies upon the ice-fields, but only the sons of the Wolf still drew breath.

Bjorn stood unmoving, the steam from his vents drifting like smoke. He said nothing. For a long moment, he simply watched as ice blasted up into their faces as the retro thrusters fired. One by one they pulled themselves up, and then Bjorn stalked up the ramp, the metal groaning beneath his weight.

They lifted off, the craft shuddering in the storm.

There was another long silence as the mists pressed close. ‘Hrutur Redmane,’ Bjorn boomed.

Hrutur stood, eager. ‘Yes!’

‘Do your folk still make winter camp around the Hollow Rock?’

‘Aye. We followed the herds between the Hollow Rock and Brondings Torp.’

‘Brondings Torp? Where is that?’

‘Three days south along the Frozen River. There was the frozen remains of an oak grove.’

There was a long pause. ‘I think I remember. That was the heart of the Brandingas… Do they still hunt those lands?’

Hrutur shook his head. ‘Never heard of them.’

Bjorn went on to explain the contours of the Brandingas land.

‘You’re sure?’ Hrutur said.

‘Yes!’ Bjorn boomed. ‘There was a pretty young lass who was daughter of their chieftain. A battle-blossom – a fierce, wolf-eyed winter rose! I told my mother I would grow up and marry her, but the prey seldom runs in the direction the pack expect…’

Hrutur shook his head. ‘No. That is all our land.’

Bjorn fell silent once more.

Hrutur did not want the talk to end. ‘There is a mound named Brandhaug on the plains above Storesness.’

‘Brandhaug?’

Hrutur spoke of the place: a low mound set high on the plateau above the forests of Dýrviðr. ‘I remember the Beast Wood,’ he said. ‘There was good hunting there.’

Hrutur’s voice faltered, retreating into silence, and Bjorn fell quiet too, his thoughts straying to the Brandingas – and to the girl with the sky-blue eyes.

Her skin had been pale as the winter sun, freckles scattered like snow-dust on stone. Her hair, dark as charred wood, had spilled long and untamed when she loosed it to the wind. And her eyes… not soft and warm, as Bjorn’s mother’s had been, but sharp and cold, cutting like glacier-light. That girl had not been gentle. She was wild, fierce, a storm given flesh. A woman whose face had haunted the firelight.

There was another long pause. Bjorn’s lights flickered.

At last Hrutur spoke up. ‘Do you sleep, Ancient One?’

‘No!’ Bjorn rumbled from his sarcophagus. ‘I was remembering. It is a common fault among the old.’
